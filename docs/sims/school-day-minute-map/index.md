## About This MicroSim

A school day feels like seven hours of teaching. This MicroSim asks you to take one illustrative middle-school day apart and see where the 425 minutes actually go.

The schedule has seven 50-minute periods, homeroom, lunch, and passing time. Periods 1 to 5 are academic. Each runs 5 minutes of settling and attendance, 20 minutes of whole-class direct instruction, 20 minutes of guided and independent practice, and 5 minutes of wrap-up. Periods 6 and 7 are art and physical education.

| Category | Minutes | Share of 425 |
|---|---|---|
| Whole-class direct instruction | 100 | 23.5% |
| Guided and independent practice | 100 | 23.5% |
| Arts and physical education | 100 | 23.5% |
| Settling, attendance, and wrap-up | 50 | 11.8% |
| Passing time | 35 | 8.2% |
| Lunch | 30 | 7.1% |
| Homeroom | 10 | 2.4% |

You make two estimates before you see the chart. The chart then shows the true totals beside your estimate. Whole-class direct instruction is less than a quarter of the day. The non-instructional minutes add up to 125, more than direct instruction.

The schedule is an illustrative composite, not a real school's timetable. Three categories tie at 100 minutes, so any of the three counts as a correct answer to estimate (b).

## How to Use

- Read the schedule on the left.
- Type estimate **(a)**, the minutes of whole-class direct instruction, and choose estimate **(b)**, the largest single use of time.
- Press **Reveal the chart**. Your estimates are scored once and cannot be changed.
- Click any bar to highlight the schedule rows that add up to it.
- **Try again** clears your estimates and hides the chart.

Nothing moves until you act, and the MicroSim does not store your answers.

## Lesson Plan

### Learning Objective

After using this MicroSim, the learner can **differentiate** the minutes of an illustrative middle-school day into whole-class direct instruction, practice, and non-instructional time, and estimate the direct-instruction total to within 10 minutes. (Bloom level: Analyze.)

### Audience

Teachers, administrators, school board members, policymakers, and parents.

### Duration

10-15 minutes.

### Prerequisites

- School day, direct instruction, the I-do, we-do, you-do pattern, and classroom, from [Chapter 1](../../chapters/01-building-blocks-schools-students/index.md).

### Activities

1. **Estimate (3 min).** Read the schedule and make both estimates before you reveal anything.
2. **Analyze (5 min).** Reveal the chart. Click each bar. Add up the four non-instructional categories yourself and compare the total with direct instruction.
3. **Discuss (5 min).** If an AI tutor took over some practice time, which category would change? Which minutes in this day could a school get back without cutting any teaching?

### Assessment

Ask learners to answer without the MicroSim.

1. How many minutes of whole-class direct instruction are in the 425-minute day? *(100 minutes, about 23.5%.)*
2. How many minutes are non-instructional (settling and wrap-up, passing, lunch, homeroom)? *(125 minutes.)*
3. Why is the direct-instruction total not 7 times 50 minutes? *(Only five of the seven periods are academic, and only 20 minutes of each academic period is whole-class explanation.)*
