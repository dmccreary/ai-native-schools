## About This MicroSim

When technology came first in schools, things often went wrong, and each failure teaches something different. The MicroSim shows seven failures, one at a time: the Los Angeles iPad plan, the AI chatbot whose startup collapsed, a microschool network that closed, student walkouts over screen time, a student data project that closed after parents objected, a vendor data breach, and AI-writing detectors that flagged non-native English writers.

You match each failure to one of seven lessons, such as "Plan the teaching before buying devices" or "Redesign assessment instead of relying on detectors." Ask what caused the failure, not what technology was involved. Each lesson matches a practice from an earlier chapter. Check figures against the sources.

## How to Use

1. Read the failure in the white box.
2. Click the lesson you think fits. The button turns blue.
3. Press **Submit**. The box below the failure says whether you are right and why.
4. If you are wrong, the button you chose turns orange. Choose again and press **Submit**. The second try does not change the score. After a second miss, the right answer turns green.
5. Press **Next** to go on. After the last failure, press **Finish** to see your score, with each failure paired with its lesson.
6. Press **Start over** at any time to reshuffle and begin again.

Only your first try on each failure counts toward the score. The failures are shuffled each run. Nothing moves until you act, and the MicroSim does not store your answers.

## Lesson Plan

### Learning Objective

After using this MicroSim, the learner can **classify** seven real failures as teaching one of seven lessons, correctly matching at least six of seven on the first attempt. (Bloom level: Understand.)

### Audience

Teachers, school administrators, school board members, education policymakers, and parents.

### Duration

10-15 minutes

### Prerequisites

- The seven cases and their lessons, from the section "When Technology Came First" in Chapter 23.

### Activities

1. **Recall (2 min).** Name as many of the seven cases as you can.
2. **Classify (6 min).** Match all seven failures. Aim for 6 of 7 on the first try.
3. **Connect (5 min).** For each lesson, name the chapter of this book that shows how to avoid the failure.

### Assessment

Ask learners to answer without the MicroSim, then check each answer by using it.

1. A district committed about $6 million to an AI chatbot startup that collapsed. Which lesson? *(Set procurement rules and check a vendor's survival.)*
2. Were these failures caused by weak technology? *(Mostly not. They were caused by planning, procurement, culture, trust, and assessment choices.)*
3. Is a data breach only a security failure? *(No. It also teaches that holding fewer records makes a smaller target.)*
