## About This MicroSim

Michael Fullan observed that performance often falls before it rises when a school adopts a new practice: the *implementation dip*. A dip is expected, but not every dip is normal, and no dip at all can be a warning that practice did not really change. This MicroSim shows three pilot series, each starting at a baseline of 70 percent correct, and a rule for judging them.

For each series you commit the month of the lowest point and a judgment, then submit. The sim then marks the low point in red and the recovery month in green, and shows the depth. Series C looks the safest, yet the rule flags it for a different reason. The data and the rule are illustrative, not research findings.

| Judgment | When it applies |
|---|---|
| Normal dip | The lowest point is in months 2 to 4, it is no deeper than 15 percent of the baseline, and it is back at or above the baseline by month 8. |
| Deep or late: investigate | The dip is deeper than 15 percent, bottoms after month 4, or has not recovered by month 8. |
| No dip: check whether practice really changed | The series never falls below 97 percent of the baseline. |

## How to Use

- Read the rule at the top. Hover over any point to read its value.
- For each series, choose the **lowest month** and a **judgment**, then press **Submit**. You get one attempt per series.
- The sim reveals the low point, the depth, and the recovery month, and explains the correct judgment.
- On a narrow screen, use the **Show** menu to switch between the series. **Start over** clears every answer.

### Good to Know

- Depth = (baseline minus lowest value) divided by baseline. The recovery month is the first month at or above the baseline after the low point.
- Mastery is 3 of 3 judgments correct. Looking at a chart after you submit is exploration. Nothing moves until you act.

## Lesson Plan

### Learning Objective

The learner will judge whether each of three pilot performance series shows a normal implementation dip, an unusually deep or late dip, or no dip, applying the stated rule, and correctly judging all three. (Bloom level: Evaluate.)

### Audience

Teachers, school administrators, school board members, education policymakers, and parents.

### Duration

10-15 minutes

### Prerequisites

- The implementation dip, managing expectations, and the rule for a normal dip, from the section "Fullan, the Implementation Dip, and CBAM" in Chapter 16.

### Activities

1. **Predict (2 min).** Before reading the charts, which is worse: a deep dip or no dip at all? Write your answer.
2. **Judge (8 min).** For each series, read the low month, compute the depth, and find the recovery month before you submit.
3. **Discuss (5 min).** What would you tell a school board that sees Series C and calls the pilot a success?

### Assessment

Ask learners to answer without the MicroSim, then check each answer by using it.

1. Series A falls from 70 to 63 at month 3. What is the depth? *(10 percent, which is within the 15 percent limit.)*
2. Series B bottoms at 54 in month 5. Why is it deep or late? *(The low comes after month 4 and the depth, 22.9 percent, is over 15 percent.)*
3. Does any drop below baseline mean a pilot is failing? *(No. A shallow, early dip with recovery by month 8 is normal.)*
4. Does no drop mean the pilot is going well? *(Not necessarily. It can mean practice did not really change.)*
