## About This MicroSim

Schools have been promised a technology revolution for more than a century. This timeline places twenty events from 1848 to 2022 in three groups:

- **Structure:** how schools are organized, such as age grading, the Carnegie unit, and virtual schools.
- **Policy:** laws and court rulings, such as Brown v. Board of Education and the Every Student Succeeds Act.
- **Technology:** tools promised to change teaching, from Edison's films and Pressey's testing machine to ChatGPT.

Shaded bands mark five eras: 1840 to 1909, 1910 to 1959, 1960 to 1989, 1990 to 2009, and 2010 to the present.

Look at where each group falls. The structures that still shape the school day, such as age-graded classrooms and the Carnegie unit, were set in the first era. The technology waves came later, and each one met those same structures.

Dates come from the chapter. They should be checked against primary sources before the book is published.

## How to Use

- **Explore.** Click any event to read its description, its date, and its era.
- Use the **Structure**, **Policy**, and **Technology** checkboxes to show or hide a group.
- **Zoom in**, **Zoom out**, the arrow buttons, and **Fit all** change the view. You can also drag the timeline. The mouse wheel does not zoom, so the page scrolls normally.
- Press **Start the era quiz**. The timeline is hidden, and eight events appear one at a time without dates, in a shuffled order. Choose an era for each. You have one attempt per event.
- After eight events you see your score and any misplaced events with their dates. **Explore the timeline** brings the timeline back. **Retry the quiz** reshuffles the events.

Mastery is 7 of 8 on the first try. The MicroSim does not store your answers.

## Lesson Plan

### Learning Objective

After using this MicroSim, the learner can **classify** eight events from the history of schooling and educational technology into one of five eras, with at least 7 of 8 correct on the first try. (Bloom level: Understand.)

### Audience

Teachers, administrators, school board members, policymakers, and parents.

### Duration

10-15 minutes.

### Prerequisites

- The industrial-era school model, Carnegie unit, education policy, charter school, virtual school, and the history of EdTech, from [Chapter 3](../../chapters/03-school-structures-equity-industrial-era/index.md).

### Activities

1. **Predict (2 min).** Before you open the timeline, guess the decade of the first teaching machine.
2. **Explore (6 min).** Show only the Technology group, then only the Structure group. In which eras does each group cluster?
3. **Quiz (5 min).** Place the eight events in their eras.
4. **Discuss (4 min).** Edison predicted in 1913 that film would replace textbooks. Which later technologies made similar promises? What school structures did they meet?

### Assessment

Ask learners to answer without the MicroSim.

1. In which era was the Carnegie unit defined? *(Era 1, 1840 to 1909. It was defined in 1906.)*
2. Is technology in schools a recent idea? *(No. Pressey's testing machine dates from 1924 and Skinner's teaching machine from 1954.)*
3. Did age grading and the Carnegie unit come from the computer age? *(No. Both come from the first era, decades before computers.)*
