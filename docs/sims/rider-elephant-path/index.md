## About This MicroSim

The Switch framework from Chip and Dan Heath says a change needs three things. The **rider** is the thinking mind, which needs clear direction. The **elephant** is the feeling mind, which needs a reason to want the change. The **path** is the situation, which can make the right behavior easier or harder.

The MicroSim shows nine tactics for a school adopting an AI tutor, one at a time. You decide which part each tactic serves. There are three tactics for each part. The key question is whom the tactic is aimed at: the thinking mind, the feeling mind, or the situation. Three of the tactics change nothing about people and everything about the situation.

## How to Use

1. Read the tactic in the white box.
2. Click the part you think fits. The button turns blue.
3. Press **Submit**. The box below the tactic says whether you are right and why.
4. If you are wrong, the button you chose turns orange. Choose again and press **Submit**. The second try does not change the score. After a second miss, the right answer turns green.
5. Press **Next** to go on. After the last tactic, press **Finish** to see your score, with the tactics grouped by part.
6. Press **Start over** at any time to reshuffle and begin again.
7. Check **Show definitions** to read what each part means. Uncheck it to go back.

Only your first try on each tactic counts toward the score. The tactics are shuffled each run. Nothing moves until you act, and the MicroSim does not store your answers.

## Lesson Plan

### Learning Objective

After using this MicroSim, the learner can **classify** nine change tactics as directing the rider, motivating the elephant, or shaping the path, correctly placing at least eight of nine on the first attempt. (Bloom level: Understand.)

### Audience

Teachers, school administrators, school board members, education policymakers, and parents.

### Duration

10-15 minutes

### Prerequisites

- The Switch framework, direct the rider, motivate the elephant, shape the path, and the nine tactics, from the section "The Switch Framework" in Chapter 19.

### Activities

1. **Recall (2 min).** Name the three parts of the Switch framework and say whom each part is aimed at.
2. **Classify (6 min).** Sort all nine tactics. Aim for 8 of 9 on the first try.
3. **Apply (5 min).** For a change in your own school, write one tactic for each part.

### Assessment

Ask learners to answer without the MicroSim, then check each answer by using it.

1. "Fix the single sign-on so the login works on the first try." Which part does it serve? *(Shape the path. It changes the situation so the behavior is easier.)*
2. Is a vivid picture of the destination aimed at feelings? *(In this framework it directs the rider: it shows the thinking mind where the change leads.)*
3. Why is shaping the path still leadership? *(Because removing friction often changes behavior more than persuasion does.)*
