## About This MicroSim

A learning record is valuable to teachers, families, vendors, researchers, and advertisers, but not everyone should see it. The chapter's five principles of student-controlled data are **minimum necessary**, **see it**, **take it**, **limit it**, and **remove it**.

For each of eight requests you choose **Allow**, **Allow only with an agreement and family notice**, or **Do not allow**, and press **Submit**. The results screen lists your first tries and how the eight requests divide.

The answers follow the chapter's five principles, which are recommended design practice, not a statement of any one law. The requests are illustrative. Legal duties are covered in Chapter 10.

## How to Use

Nothing moves until you act, and the MicroSim does not store your answers.

- **Allow, Agreement and family notice, Do not allow:** choose a response.
- **Submit:** commits it. Two attempts per request; only the first counts.
- **Next:** moves on; after the eighth it shows your score.
- **Show the responses and principles:** checkbox that shows what each response means and the five principles.
- **Try again:** reshuffles the requests and starts over.

Ask two questions: who is asking, and for what purpose?

## Lesson Plan

### Learning Objective

After using this MicroSim, the learner will **judge** whether each of eight requests to see or use a student's learning record should be allowed, allowed only with an agreement and family notice, or not allowed, correctly judging at least seven of eight on the first attempt. (Bloom level: Evaluate.)

### Audience

Teachers, administrators, school board members, education policymakers, and parents.

### Duration

10-15 minutes.

### Prerequisites

- Learning record, student-controlled data and its five parts, and data portability, from the section "Data Portability and Student-Controlled Data" in Chapter 8.

### Activities

1. **Review (2 min).** Read the three responses and the five principles.
2. **Judge (6 min).** For each request, name the requester and the purpose before you submit.
3. **Discuss (4 min).** A teacher appears under both Allow and Do not allow. What made the difference?

### Assessment

Ask learners to answer without the MicroSim, then check each answer by using it.

1. A teacher views her own class's mastery records to plan. Response? *(Allow. She has a direct educational need.)*
2. The vendor wants identifiable records to train a new commercial product. Response? *(Do not allow. The use goes beyond the service the school bought.)*
3. A district-approved tutoring partner asks for one student's fractions mastery. Response? *(Allow only with an agreement and family notice.)*
4. May a family take a copy of the record? *(Yes. Data portability lets the record follow the student.)*
