## About This MicroSim

A teammate says, "I'm not going to present. I always mess it up in front of people." There are many ways to reply. Three of them are **active listening**:

- **Paraphrase**: restates what the speaker said in your own words.
- **Clarifying question**: asks the speaker to say more about what they mean.
- **Reflecting feeling**: names the feeling the speaker seems to have.

Two common replies are not listening, however kind they sound:

- **Advice**: tells the speaker what to do.
- **Judgment**: evaluates or argues with what the speaker said.

Classify each reply by what it does, not by its tone. The statement and replies are illustrative examples written for this chapter.

## How to Use

Read the reply, then choose one of the five kinds. Press **Show definitions** to see what each kind does. A wrong first answer gives a hint and lets you choose again, but only the first answer counts toward **First-try correct**. The feedback also says whether the reply is active listening. After eight replies, the results screen shows your score and how many replies were active listening.

### Controls

- **Choice buttons:** commit an answer. A wrong first answer gives a hint and lets you choose again; only the first answer counts.
- **Show definitions:** opens or closes the reference table. Reading it is exploration, not evidence.
- **Next:** moves to the next item after feedback. On the results screen, Previous missed and Next missed step through what you missed.
- **Play again:** starts a practice run with the items reshuffled. Practice runs do not change the first-run score.

Nothing moves until you act, and the MicroSim does not store your answers.

## Lesson Plan

### Learning Objective

After using this MicroSim, the learner can **classify** eight replies to a teammate's statement as paraphrase, clarifying question, reflecting feeling, advice, or judgment, correctly placing at least seven of eight on the first attempt. (Bloom level: Understand.)

### Audience

Teachers, administrators, school board members, policymakers, and parents.

### Duration

10 minutes

### Prerequisites

- Active listening and its parts (paraphrase, clarifying question, reflecting feeling), advice, and judgment, from the section "Communication, Listening, and Conflict" in Chapter 13

### Activities

1. **Classify (5 min).** Place the eight replies. Ask: does this reply help the teammate say more, or does it steer them?
2. **Notice (2 min).** "Just practice more and you'll be fine" is kind. Why is it still advice?
3. **Practice (5 min).** In pairs, one person states a worry. The other answers with a paraphrase, then a clarifying question, then a reflected feeling.

### Assessment

Mastery is 7 of 8 correct on the first try. Only the first answer to each reply on the first run counts. Then ask learners to answer these without the MicroSim:

1. "Have you tried writing notes on cards?" Which kind? *(Advice. It is a question, but it tells the speaker what to do.)*
2. Is agreeing with someone the same as understanding them? *(No. A paraphrase checks understanding; agreeing does not.)*
3. Name the three kinds of reply that are active listening. *(Paraphrase, clarifying question, and reflecting feeling.)*
