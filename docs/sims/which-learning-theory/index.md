## About This MicroSim

Four learning theories have similar names and different defining features:

| Theory | Defining feature |
|---|---|
| Constructivism | A learner builds new understanding from what they already know and revises their own ideas |
| Social constructivism | A learner builds understanding with help from others, within reach of what they can do alone |
| Constructionism | A learner learns especially well by making a tangible, shareable artifact |
| Social constructionism | A group learns by building a shared artifact together |

Two questions sort them. Is something being made? Is a helper or a group involved? The scenarios come in near pairs, such as a catapult built alone and a dashboard built by a team, so you have to find the defining feature. The scenarios are illustrative examples written for this chapter.

## How to Use

Read the scenario, then choose one of the four theories. Press **Show definitions** to see each theory's defining feature. A wrong first answer gives a hint and lets you choose again, but only the first answer counts toward **First-try correct**. After eight scenarios, the results screen shows your score and the scenarios you misplaced.

### Controls

- **Choice buttons:** commit an answer. A wrong first answer gives a hint and lets you choose again; only the first answer counts.
- **Show definitions:** opens or closes the reference table. Reading it is exploration, not evidence.
- **Next:** moves to the next item after feedback. On the results screen, Previous missed and Next missed step through what you missed.
- **Play again:** starts a practice run with the items reshuffled. Practice runs do not change the first-run score.

Nothing moves until you act, and the MicroSim does not store your answers.

## Lesson Plan

### Learning Objective

After using this MicroSim, the learner can **classify** eight classroom scenarios as constructivism, social constructivism, constructionism, or social constructionism, correctly placing at least seven of eight on the first attempt. (Bloom level: Understand.)

### Audience

Teachers, administrators, school board members, policymakers, and parents.

### Duration

10 minutes

### Prerequisites

- Constructivism, social constructivism, zone of proximal development, constructionism, and social constructionism, from the section "How Students Learn: Four Theories" in Chapter 13

### Activities

1. **Classify (5 min).** Place the eight scenarios. For each one, answer the two sorting questions before you choose.
2. **Compare (3 min).** The catapult and the food-waste dashboard both involve building. What single detail separates them?
3. **Apply (4 min).** Describe one afternoon project at your school. Which theory does it draw on most?

### Assessment

Mastery is 7 of 8 correct on the first try. Only the first answer to each scenario on the first run counts. Then ask learners to answer these without the MicroSim:

1. Are constructivism and constructionism the same? *(No. Constructionism adds making a tangible artifact.)*
2. Is every group activity social constructivism? *(No. If the group builds a shared artifact, it is social constructionism.)*
3. A student builds a model bridge alone and improves it after a test. Which theory? *(Constructionism.)*
