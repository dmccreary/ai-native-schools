## About This MicroSim

Schools and vendors make claims about results. Six questions expose most weak claims:

1. Where did the students start?
2. Compared with whom?
3. Who measured it, and who paid?
4. Has anyone outside checked it?
5. How big, and for how long?
6. What was actually measured?

The MicroSim shows six claims, one at a time. Each one is built to be missing one thing: a starting point, a comparison, an independent measure, an outside check, a size, or the thing measured. You pick the question that exposes it. Every claim sounds impressive until the right question is asked. The claims are illustrative, written in the style of real claims.

## How to Use

1. Read the claim in the white box.
2. Click the question you think fits. The button turns blue.
3. Press **Submit**. The box below the claim says whether you are right and why.
4. If you are wrong, the button you chose turns orange. Choose again and press **Submit**. The second try does not change the score. After a second miss, the right answer turns green.
5. Press **Next** to go on. After the last claim, press **Finish** to see your score, with each claim and its best question.
6. Press **Start over** at any time to reshuffle and begin again.

Only your first try on each claim counts toward the score. The claims are shuffled each run. Nothing moves until you act, and the MicroSim does not store your answers.

## Lesson Plan

### Learning Objective

After using this MicroSim, the learner can **classify** six result claims by the question that best exposes the weakness in each, correctly matching at least five of six on the first attempt. (Bloom level: Understand.)

### Audience

Teachers, school administrators, school board members, education policymakers, and parents.

### Duration

10 minutes

### Prerequisites

- The six questions for evaluating result claims, from the section "Evaluating Result Claims" in Chapter 23, and Chapters 2 and 3.

### Activities

1. **Recall (2 min).** Say the six questions without looking.
2. **Classify (5 min).** Match all six claims. Aim for 5 of 6 on the first try.
3. **Apply (5 min).** Find a real result claim from a vendor or a news story and ask all six questions of it.

### Assessment

Ask learners to answer without the MicroSim, then check each answer by using it.

1. "94 percent of teachers said they love the program." Which question exposes it? *(What was actually measured? Satisfaction is not learning.)*
2. Is any claim with a number evidence? *(No. A number without a starting point, a comparison, or an outside check proves little.)*
3. "A new preprint shows an AI tutor doubles learning." Which question? *(Has anyone outside checked it? A preprint has not been peer reviewed.)*
