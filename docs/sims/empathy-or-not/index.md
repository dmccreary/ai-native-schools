## About This MicroSim

A teacher says, "I'm afraid this means my job won't exist in five years." The MicroSim shows eight replies to that one statement, one at a time. You sort each reply into one of four kinds:

| Kind | What it does |
|---|---|
| Dismissive | Tells the person the worry is unfounded or silly |
| Defensive | Argues with the worry or restates the plan without acknowledging the feeling |
| Reassurance without substance | Offers comfort with no facts, options, or commitments |
| Empathic and substantive | Acknowledges the concern and then offers facts, options, or a commitment |

Each kind has two replies. Some kind-sounding replies turn out to be empty, and some factual replies turn out to be defensive. Only the empathic and substantive replies both acknowledge the worry and offer something concrete.

## How to Use

1. Read the reply in the white box.
2. Click the kind of reply you think fits. The button turns blue.
3. Press **Submit**. The box below the reply says whether you are right and why.
4. If you are wrong, the button you chose turns orange. Choose again and press **Submit**. The second try does not change the score. After a second miss, the right answer turns green.
5. Press **Next** to go on. After the last reply, press **Finish** to see your score, with the replies grouped by kind.
6. Press **Start over** at any time to reshuffle and begin again.
7. Check **Show definitions** to read what each kind of reply means. Uncheck it to go back.

Only your first try on each reply counts toward the score. The replys are shuffled each run. Nothing moves until you act, and the MicroSim does not store your answers.

## Lesson Plan

### Learning Objective

After using this MicroSim, the learner can **classify** eight replies to a worried teacher as dismissive, defensive, reassurance without substance, or empathic and substantive, correctly placing at least seven of eight on the first attempt. (Bloom level: Understand.)

### Audience

Teachers, school administrators, school board members, education policymakers, and parents.

### Duration

10-15 minutes

### Prerequisites

- Responding with empathy, legitimate concern, and fear of job loss, from the section "Resistance, Concerns, and Job Fears" in Chapter 19.

### Activities

1. **Predict (2 min).** Before you start, say what makes "Don't worry, it'll all work out" different from an empathic reply.
2. **Classify (6 min).** Sort all eight replies. Aim for 7 of 8 on the first try.
3. **Rewrite (5 min).** Pick one dismissive or defensive reply and rewrite it so it acknowledges the worry and offers a fact, an option, or a commitment.

### Assessment

Ask learners to answer without the MicroSim, then check each answer by using it.

1. "I understand, I'm sure you'll be fine." What kind of reply is this? *(Reassurance without substance. It sounds kind but offers nothing concrete.)*
2. Is every reply that disagrees with the teacher defensive? *(No. A reply can disagree with the facts and still acknowledge the feeling and offer support.)*
3. What two things does an empathic and substantive reply do? *(It acknowledges the concern, and then it offers facts, options, or a commitment.)*
