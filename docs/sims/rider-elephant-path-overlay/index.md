## About This MicroSim

Chip and Dan Heath's *Switch* (2010) describes a change as a rider sitting on an elephant, on a path. The rider is the thinking, planning side of a person. The elephant is the feeling side: much stronger, and the part that tires of effort and balks. The path is the situation around them. This infographic draws that image and places each of the framework's nine moves in the scene as an object you can find.

The rider holds a map and a scroll and points toward a school on the far hilltop. The elephant looks tired, with a heart floating near it, a medal on its harness and one small stone in front of its foot. The path is fenced and worn smooth, with a blank signpost beside it and a herd already walking ahead. The overall callout names the risk the chapter warns about: a clear plan for the rider, with an unmotivated elephant, stalls. The response is to address all three parts.

The companion MicroSim [Rider, Elephant, or Path?](../rider-elephant-path/index.md) asks you to sort the same nine moves, written as school tactics, into the three parts.

## How to Use

- **Explore:** hover over a numbered marker or a label to read about that part of the scene. Each move includes a school example as a tip.
- **Quiz:** click **Quiz**. The sim names a move, and you click the marker where it lives. Your score shows next to the buttons.
- **Calibrate (authors):** open [`main.html?edit=true`](./main.html?edit=true) to drag markers, then copy the JSON into `data.json`.

## The 10 Callouts

1. **Rider, elephant and path: all three**: the overall idea, its risk and the response
2. **Follow the bright spots** (direct the rider): the glowing patch on the far hillside
3. **Script the critical moves** (direct the rider): the scroll in the rider's back hand
4. **Point to the destination** (direct the rider): the school on the hilltop
5. **Find the feeling** (motivate the elephant): the heart floating near the elephant
6. **Shrink the change** (motivate the elephant): the small stone in front of its foot
7. **Grow your people** (motivate the elephant): the medal on the harness
8. **Tweak the environment** (shape the path): the signpost beside the road
9. **Build habits** (shape the path): the worn groove in the road
10. **Rally the herd** (shape the path): the elephants walking ahead

The scene is a text-free SVG made by `make-rider-elephant-svg.py` in this folder. The script also prints the marker coordinates used in `data.json`.

## Lesson Plan

### Learning Objective

The learner will locate the nine moves of the Switch framework on a picture of the rider, the elephant and the path, and explain why a change stalls when only one of the three parts is addressed.

### Audience

School and district leaders, teachers and instructional coaches.

### Duration

10 minutes.

### Activities

1. **Explore (5 minutes):** hover over all 10 callouts. Notice which three objects belong to the rider, which three to the elephant and which three to the path.
2. **Quiz (3 minutes):** run Quiz mode until you can find every callout without hovering first.
3. **Apply (2 minutes):** think of a change that has stalled in your own school. Is the problem the rider, the elephant or the path? Name one small move that would help.

### Assessment

The learner finds at least 9 of 10 callouts in Quiz mode and can diagnose a stalled change as a rider, elephant or path problem, with one matching move.
