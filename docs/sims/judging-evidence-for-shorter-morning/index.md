## About This MicroSim

The case for a shorter academic morning rests on claims of very uneven strength. This MicroSim gives you eight of them and asks you to place each at one of the five evidence levels from Chapter 2:

| Level | What it requires |
|---|---|
| Strong | Random assignment, well designed and carried out |
| Moderate | A comparison group, no random assignment |
| Promising | A correlational study with statistical controls |
| Demonstrates a rationale | A research-based reason; results not yet in |
| No evidence of effect | Testimonials, surveys, self-reported or uncontrolled results |

Some claims support this book's own proposal and still sit at the bottom level. Judge the design of the study, not whether you agree with the result. The claims are summaries of the studies and reports described in Chapter 11 and the AI-First Stories appendix; check the sources before citing them.

## How to Use

Read the claim, then choose one of the five levels below it. Press **Show definitions** to see what each level requires. A wrong first answer gives a hint and lets you choose again, but only the first answer counts toward **First-try correct**. After eight claims, the results screen shows your score and the claims you misplaced, with the correct level and the reason.

### Controls

- **Choice buttons:** commit an answer. A wrong first answer gives a hint and lets you choose again; only the first answer counts.
- **Show definitions:** opens or closes the reference table. Reading it is exploration, not evidence.
- **Next:** moves to the next item after feedback. On the results screen, Previous missed and Next missed step through what you missed.
- **Play again:** starts a practice run with the items reshuffled. Practice runs do not change the first-run score.

Nothing moves until you act, and the MicroSim does not store your answers.

## Lesson Plan

### Learning Objective

After using this MicroSim, the learner can **judge** the strength of the evidence behind eight claims about AI tutoring and the compressed morning by assigning each to one of the five evidence levels from Chapter 2, correctly placing at least seven of eight on the first attempt. (Bloom level: Evaluate.)

### Audience

Teachers, administrators, school board members, policymakers, and parents.

### Duration

15 minutes

### Prerequisites

- The five evidence levels, randomized controlled trial, and self-reported results, from Chapters 2 and 3
- The studies described in the section "AI Tutors" in Chapter 11

### Activities

1. **Judge (8 min).** Place the eight claims. For each one, ask first: was there random assignment? Then: was there any comparison group?
2. **Notice (3 min).** Which claims reached Strong? What do they have in common? Is any claim about whole-school results Strong?
3. **Discuss (4 min).** A board member quotes a school's reported gains as proof. Using this MicroSim, explain what kind of evidence would change your mind.

### Assessment

Mastery is 7 of 8 correct on the first try. Only the first answer to each claim on the first run counts. Then ask learners to answer these without the MicroSim:

1. A school reports its own test gains with no comparison group. Which level? *(No evidence of effect.)*
2. Is a published meta-analysis the same as a randomized trial? *(No. It pools studies, and if most were not randomized, it is Moderate at best.)*
3. A tutor is built on solid research but has not been evaluated yet. Which level? *(Demonstrates a rationale.)*
