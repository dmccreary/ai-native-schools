## About This MicroSim

John Kotter's eight steps describe what leaders do, in order, to lead a large change: from creating a sense of urgency to anchoring the change in culture. You read eight actions from one school's change effort and choose the step each one carries out. Each step has exactly one action.

Some actions sound alike but serve different purposes. Sharing a pilot's results is a short-term win; using those results to add two more grades is building on change. On a wide screen, the results screen lists the steps in order, so the eight actions read as the story of one change. The actions are illustrative, written for this chapter.

| Step | Definition |
|---|---|
| 1. Sense of urgency | Enough people believe change is needed now |
| 2. Guiding coalition | A group with the power and trust to lead the change |
| 3. Change vision | A clear picture of the future the change creates |
| 4. Communicating the vision | Repeating the vision in many ways and actions |
| 5. Removing obstacles | Clearing barriers in structures, systems, and skills |
| 6. Short-term wins | Early, visible successes |
| 7. Building on change | Using early success to take on bigger problems |
| 8. Anchoring in culture | Making the new way part of the normal way |

## How to Use

- Read the action on the card. The progress strip shows which action you are on and your running score, "First-try correct".
- Choose one of the eight steps with the buttons. If your first answer is wrong, a hint appears and you choose again. Only your first answer counts toward the score.
- **Show definitions** opens a table of the steps. Reading it is exploration, not evidence of mastery.
- **Next** moves on after you read the feedback. On the results screen, **Previous missed** and **Next missed** page through the ones you missed, and **Play again** reshuffles the actions for a practice run.

### Good to Know

- Mastery is 7 of 8 correct on the first try, on the first run. Practice runs give the same feedback but do not change the first-run score.
- The actions are shuffled once per run. Nothing moves until you act, and the MicroSim does not store your answers.

## Lesson Plan

### Learning Objective

The learner will classify eight leadership actions by the Kotter step each carries out, correctly placing at least seven of eight on the first attempt. (Bloom level: Understand.)

### Audience

Teachers, school administrators, school board members, education policymakers, and parents.

### Duration

10-15 minutes

### Prerequisites

- Kotter's eight steps, from the section "Kotter: From Urgency to Anchoring" in Chapter 17.

### Activities

1. **Recall (2 min).** List the eight steps in order from memory.
2. **Classify (8 min).** Work through the eight actions. Ask what purpose each action serves before you choose.
3. **Discuss (5 min).** Read the results screen in step order. Which step does your own school most often skip?

### Assessment

Ask learners to answer without the MicroSim, then check each answer by using it.

1. The vision is repeated at every staff meeting, in newsletters, and at family nights. Which step? *(Communicating the vision.)*
2. The district fixes the login problem that blocked teachers. Which step? *(Removing obstacles.)*
3. Does a short-term win mean the change is finished? *(No. Step 7 uses early wins to take on bigger problems.)*
4. Is communicating the vision a single announcement? *(No. It is repetition through many channels and actions.)*
