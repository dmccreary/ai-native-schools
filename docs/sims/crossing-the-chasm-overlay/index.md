## About This MicroSim

This infographic draws Rogers' adoption curve as a landscape. The ground rises and falls in the shape of the bell curve, so each adopter group stands on its share of the curve. Geoffrey Moore's *chasm* is a deep canyon between the early adopters (visionaries) and the early majority (pragmatists). The far rim is higher than the near cliff, because pragmatists hold an innovation to a higher standard than visionaries do.

The scene also shows Moore's two main ideas for getting across: a half-built bridge stands for the **whole product**, and a landing boat stands for the **beachhead**. A figure leaping off the end of the unfinished bridge shows the risk the chapter calls *crossing by hope*: spreading a pilot, unchanged, to everyone.

## How to Use

- **Explore:** hover over a numbered marker or a label to read about that part of the scene. Many callouts include a tip at the bottom.
- **Quiz:** click **Quiz**. The sim names a part of the scene, and you click the marker where it lives. Your score shows next to the buttons.
- **Calibrate (authors):** open [`main.html?edit=true`](./main.html?edit=true) to drag markers, then copy the JSON into `data.json`.

## The 13 Callouts

1. **Innovators (about 2.5%)**: technology enthusiasts, carrying lanterns
2. **Crack 1**: the small gap between innovators and early adopters
3. **Early adopters: visionaries (about 13.5%)**: figures with telescopes on the ridge
4. **The chasm**: the canyon between early adopters and the early majority
5. **Whole product**: the half-built bridge, one plank per needed piece
6. **Crossing by hope**: the risk of spreading an unchanged pilot to everyone
7. **Beachhead**: the landing boat on one small beach
8. **Bowling pins**: each group won helps win the next, similar group
9. **References**: pragmatists listening to other pragmatists
10. **Early majority: pragmatists (about 34%)**: the broad slope up from the far rim
11. **Crack 2**: the gap between the early and the late majority
12. **Late majority: conservatives (about 34%)**: the slope down from the summit
13. **Laggards: skeptics (about 16%)**: a few figures standing apart

The landscape image is a text-free SVG made by `make-chasm-svg.py` in this folder, so the ground line follows the bell curve exactly. If you prefer a painted look, `image-prompt.md` describes the same scene for a text-to-image tool.

## Lesson Plan

### Learning Objective

The learner will identify the five adopter groups, the chasm, the whole product and the beachhead on a picture of the adoption curve, and explain why an innovation can stall between early adopters and the early majority.

### Audience

School and district leaders, teachers and instructional coaches.

### Duration

10 minutes.

### Activities

1. **Explore (5 minutes):** hover over all 13 callouts. Note the two tips about visionaries and references.
2. **Quiz (3 minutes):** run Quiz mode until you can find every callout without hovering first.
3. **Apply (2 minutes):** name one innovation in your own school. Which group has it reached? What planks of its bridge are still missing?

### Assessment

The learner finds at least 11 of 13 callouts in Quiz mode and can name two pieces of the whole product their own innovation still lacks.
