## About This MicroSim

Geoffrey Moore's *chasm* is the gap between early adopters, who like new things, and the early majority, pragmatists who want a complete solution that people like them already use. Each of six situations shows an innovation stuck at the chasm. You choose the move that best helps it cross: choose a beachhead, build the whole product, use a trusted reference, or add more features.

To choose well, first diagnose what the pragmatists lack: a focus, a complete solution, or a trusted peer. "Add more features" is never the best move in this set. The situations are illustrative, written for this chapter.

| Move | Definition |
|---|---|
| Choose a beachhead | Focus all effort on one narrow, well-defined group and make the innovation work completely there first |
| Build the whole product | Add the training, integration, support, and safeguards that make it work in practice |
| Use a trusted reference | Let a respected peer from a similar setting tell the story |
| Add more features | Extend what the product can do for the most enthusiastic users |

## How to Use

- Read the situation on the card. The progress strip shows which situation you are on and your running score, "First-try correct".
- Choose one of the four moves with the buttons. If your first answer is wrong, a hint appears and you choose again. Only your first answer counts toward the score.
- **Show definitions** opens a table of the moves. Reading it is exploration, not evidence of mastery.
- **Next** moves on after you read the feedback. On the results screen, **Previous missed** and **Next missed** page through the ones you missed, and **Play again** reshuffles the situations for a practice run.

### Good to Know

- Mastery is 5 of 6 correct on the first try, on the first run. Practice runs give the same feedback but do not change the first-run score.
- The situations are shuffled once per run. Nothing moves until you act, and the MicroSim does not store your answers.

## Lesson Plan

### Learning Objective

The learner will apply Moore's chasm ideas by choosing, for each of six situations, the move that best helps an innovation cross from early adopters to the early majority, correctly choosing at least five of six on the first attempt. (Bloom level: Apply.)

### Audience

Teachers, school administrators, school board members, education policymakers, and parents.

### Duration

10-15 minutes

### Prerequisites

- The chasm, pragmatist buyers, crossing the chasm, the beachhead strategy, the whole product, and opinion leaders, from the sections "Adopter Categories and the Adoption Curve" and "The Chasm" in Chapter 15.

### Activities

1. **Predict (2 min).** Which move do vendors most often reach for when adoption stalls? Write your guess.
2. **Apply (8 min).** Work through the six situations. For each one, say what the early majority is missing before you choose.
3. **Discuss (5 min).** Build the whole product is the best move in three of the six situations. Why does it come up so often in schools?

### Assessment

Ask learners to answer without the MicroSim, then check each answer by using it.

1. A district gave a little support to all 40 schools at once and none has a visible success. Which move? *(Choose a beachhead: focus on one group first.)*
2. Teachers say the tool has no training and does not connect to their login. Which move? *(Build the whole product.)*
3. Will more features persuade pragmatists? *(No. Pragmatists need a complete, supported solution and a trusted reference.)*
4. A skeptical teacher asks, "Has anyone like me made this work?" Which move? *(Use a trusted reference.)*
