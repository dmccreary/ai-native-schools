## About This MicroSim

Chapter 16 introduces eight models of change. Each one answers a different question, from "What should leaders do, and in what order?" (Kotter) to "Where is each teacher, in concerns and in use?" (CBAM). You read eight situations, one per model, and choose the model that best addresses each one.

The trick is to find the question the situation is really asking: sequence, phase, an individual's gap, feelings, culture, learning capacity, the school-specific dip, or teacher concerns. The models do not compete; each is best for a different question. The situations are illustrative, written for this chapter.

| Model | Question it answers |
|---|---|
| Kotter's eight steps | What should leaders do, and in what order? |
| Lewin's change model | What are the basic phases of a change? |
| ADKAR | Where is this individual stuck? |
| Bridges' transition model | How are people feeling, and what do they need now? |
| Schein's culture model | Why does the organization resist what it says it wants? |
| Senge's learning organization | How do we become a place that keeps learning? |
| Fullan's educational change | What is distinctive about changing a school, and why does it dip first? |
| CBAM | Where is each teacher, in concerns and in use? |

## How to Use

- Read the situation on the card. The progress strip shows which situation you are on and your running score, "First-try correct".
- Choose one of the eight models with the buttons. If your first answer is wrong, a hint appears and you choose again. Only your first answer counts toward the score.
- **Show questions** opens a table of the models. Reading it is exploration, not evidence of mastery.
- **Next** moves on after you read the feedback. On the results screen, **Previous missed** and **Next missed** page through the ones you missed, and **Play again** reshuffles the situations for a practice run.

### Good to Know

- Mastery is 7 of 8 correct on the first try, on the first run. Practice runs give the same feedback but do not change the first-run score.
- The situations are shuffled once per run. Nothing moves until you act, and the MicroSim does not store your answers.

## Lesson Plan

### Learning Objective

The learner will classify eight school change situations by the model that best addresses them, correctly placing at least seven of eight on the first attempt. (Bloom level: Understand.)

### Audience

Teachers, school administrators, school board members, education policymakers, and parents.

### Duration

15 minutes

### Prerequisites

- The eight models (Kotter, Lewin, ADKAR, Bridges, Schein, Senge, Fullan, and CBAM), from the section "The Models at a Glance" in Chapter 16.

### Activities

1. **Recall (3 min).** Without the sim, write the question each of the eight models answers.
2. **Classify (8 min).** Work through the eight situations. Press Show questions only after you have tried to recall them.
3. **Discuss (4 min).** Pick a change your school is making now. Which two models would you use first, and why?

### Assessment

Ask learners to answer without the MicroSim, then check each answer by using it.

1. Teachers are grieving the loss of a familiar role. Which model? *(Bridges' transition model.)*
2. Scores fell before they rose and leaders are worried. Which model expects this? *(Fullan's educational change, which names the implementation dip.)*
3. Can a model for individuals, such as ADKAR, diagnose a whole culture? *(No. Schein's culture model is built for that.)*
4. Is one model right for every change problem? *(No. Each model answers a different question.)*
