## About This MicroSim

Chapter 19 describes six microstrategies, small moves a leader can make with one person or one team: a teacher champion, a pilot classroom, instructional coaching, shrinking the first step, peer observation, and a professional learning community. You read eight teacher situations and choose the microstrategy that best fits each one.

Diagnose first: what concern does the person have, and what kind of adopter are they? Then pick the move that answers it. Two situations call for coaching and two for a pilot, for different reasons. The situations are illustrative, written for this chapter.

| Microstrategy | Description |
|---|---|
| Teacher champion | A respected colleague shares honestly how it went |
| Pilot classroom | Try it in one class, with a baseline and a plan, before committing |
| Instructional coaching | A trained colleague works with the teacher over time |
| Shrink the first step | Ask for one very small first action |
| Peer observation | Watch a colleague teach, for learning, not evaluation |
| Professional learning community | A team meets weekly to examine evidence and plan |

## How to Use

- Read the situation on the card. The progress strip shows which situation you are on and your running score, "First-try correct".
- Choose one of the six microstrategies with the buttons. If your first answer is wrong, a hint appears and you choose again. Only your first answer counts toward the score.
- **Show descriptions** opens a table of the microstrategies. Reading it is exploration, not evidence of mastery.
- **Next** moves on after you read the feedback. On the results screen, **Previous missed** and **Next missed** page through the ones you missed, and **Play again** reshuffles the situations for a practice run.

### Good to Know

- Mastery is 7 of 8 correct on the first try, on the first run. Practice runs give the same feedback but do not change the first-run score.
- The situations are shuffled once per run. Nothing moves until you act, and the MicroSim does not store your answers.

## Lesson Plan

### Learning Objective

The learner will apply the matching tables by choosing the microstrategy that best fits each of eight teacher situations, correctly choosing at least seven of eight on the first attempt. (Bloom level: Apply.)

### Audience

Teachers, school administrators, school board members, education policymakers, and parents.

### Duration

15 minutes

### Prerequisites

- The six microstrategies and the matching tables in the section "Matching the Strategy to the Person" in Chapter 19.

### Activities

1. **Predict (2 min).** When teachers struggle with a new practice, what do schools usually offer? Write it down.
2. **Apply (8 min).** Work through the eight situations. Name the concern before you choose the move.
3. **Discuss (5 min).** The two coaching situations call for coaching for different reasons. What are they?

### Assessment

Ask learners to answer without the MicroSim, then check each answer by using it.

1. A teacher says, "I want to see what this looks like in a real class." Which microstrategy? *(Peer observation.)*
2. A late-majority teacher wants data from a classroom in the same building. Which microstrategy? *(Pilot classroom.)*
3. Does more training solve every concern? *(No. The move depends on the concern; an overwhelmed teacher needs a smaller first step.)*
4. Is a mandate a microstrategy? *(No. A mandate does not answer anyone's concern.)*
