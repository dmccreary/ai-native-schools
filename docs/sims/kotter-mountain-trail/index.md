## About This MicroSim

This infographic draws John Kotter's eight steps for leading change as a trail up a mountain. Each step is a camp on a switchback, so the climb has a clear order of work: a team cannot reach the higher camps without passing through the lower ones. The base camp burns a red flare for urgency, and a finished stone cairn on the summit stands for anchoring the change in the school's culture.

Two hazards show what goes wrong. A climber who takes the shortcut gully slides back to the bottom, which is what happens when a school skips early steps. On a lower false summit, a climber celebrates beside a cairn that is only half built, which is what happens when a school declares victory after its first win. The response to both is the one the chapter gives: Lewin's refreeze and Kotter's anchoring, which say that a change is not done until it is the normal way of working.

## How to Use

- **Explore:** hover over a numbered marker or a label to read about that camp or hazard. Most callouts include a tip from the chapter's Lincoln example.
- **Quiz:** click **Quiz**. The sim names a step or a risk, and you click the marker where it lives. Your score shows next to the buttons.
- **Calibrate (authors):** open [`main.html?edit=true`](./main.html?edit=true) to drag markers, then copy the JSON into `data.json`.

## The 10 Callouts

1. **Sense of urgency**: the red flare at the base camp
2. **Guiding coalition**: a rope team of four climbers
3. **Change vision**: a telescope aimed at the summit
4. **Communicating the vision**: a signal-flag station
5. **Removing obstacles**: fallen logs being cleared from the trail
6. **Short-term wins**: a small flag planted on a ledge
7. **Building on change**: a wider camp with more tents
8. **Anchoring in culture**: the finished cairn on the summit
9. **Risk: skipping steps**: a climber sliding down the shortcut gully
10. **Risk: declaring victory too soon**: a half-built cairn on a false summit

The mountain image is a text-free SVG made by `make-kotter-trail-svg.py` in this folder. The script also prints the marker positions used in `data.json`.

For practice placing real school actions into the eight steps, try the [Which Kotter Step Is This?](../which-kotter-step/index.md) MicroSim.

## Lesson Plan

### Learning Objective

The learner will identify Kotter's eight steps in order on a picture of a mountain trail, and explain why skipping a step or declaring victory too soon leaves a change effort stranded.

### Audience

School and district leaders, teachers and instructional coaches.

### Duration

10 minutes.

### Activities

1. **Explore (5 minutes):** hover over all 10 callouts in trail order, from the flare at the base to the cairn at the summit. Then read the two risks.
2. **Quiz (3 minutes):** run Quiz mode until you can find every callout without hovering first.
3. **Apply (2 minutes):** think of one change in your own school. Which camp has it reached? Is it on the trail, in the gully, or on a false summit?

### Assessment

The learner finds at least 9 of 10 callouts in Quiz mode and can name the step their own school's change effort needs next.
