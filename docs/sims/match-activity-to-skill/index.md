## About This MicroSim

A pro-social afternoon is designed around the human skills that AI does not replace. This MicroSim uses five of them:

| Skill | Definition |
|---|---|
| Collaboration | Working with others so that each contribution shapes the result |
| Communication | Expressing ideas to others and understanding theirs |
| Empathy | Understanding and sharing another person's feelings and view |
| Creativity | Producing ideas or products that are new and valuable |
| Computational thinking | Breaking problems into parts, finding patterns, abstracting, and writing precise steps |

Most activities build more than one skill. The question is which skill the activity asks for most directly. The feedback names a second skill each activity builds, so the single answer is a primary emphasis, not the only one. The activities are illustrative examples written for this chapter.

## How to Use

Read the activity, then choose one of the five skills. Press **Show definitions** to see the five skills. A wrong first answer gives a hint and lets you choose again, but only the first answer counts toward **First-try correct**. After ten activities, the results screen shows your score and the activities you misplaced.

### Controls

- **Choice buttons:** commit an answer. A wrong first answer gives a hint and lets you choose again; only the first answer counts.
- **Show definitions:** opens or closes the reference table. Reading it is exploration, not evidence.
- **Next:** moves to the next item after feedback. On the results screen, Previous missed and Next missed step through what you missed.
- **Play again:** starts a practice run with the items reshuffled. Practice runs do not change the first-run score.

Nothing moves until you act, and the MicroSim does not store your answers.

## Lesson Plan

### Learning Objective

After using this MicroSim, the learner can **classify** ten afternoon activities by the human skill each builds most directly, correctly placing at least eight of ten on the first attempt. (Bloom level: Understand.)

### Audience

Teachers, administrators, school board members, policymakers, and parents.

### Duration

10-15 minutes

### Prerequisites

- Collaboration, communication skills, empathy, creativity, computational thinking, and the activity-skill matrix, from Chapters 12, 13, and 14

### Activities

1. **Classify (6 min).** Place the ten activities. Ask: what does this activity ask students to do most of the time?
2. **Notice (2 min).** Count the chips in each bin. Read the feedback again: what second skill did each activity build?
3. **Design (5 min).** List your school's current afternoon activities. Which of the five skills is missing?

### Assessment

Mastery is 8 of 10 correct on the first try. Only the first answer to each activity on the first run counts. Then ask learners to answer these without the MicroSim:

1. A robotics team designs, builds, and debugs a robot together. Primary skill? *(Computational thinking, with collaboration as a strong second skill.)*
2. Do arts activities build only creativity? *(No. An orchestra builds collaboration first.)*
3. Do technical activities need collaboration? *(Often. Robot debugging is usually a team effort.)*
