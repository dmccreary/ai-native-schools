## About This MicroSim

Stories about AI-first schools mix promise and peril. This MicroSim uses the book's framework of two benefits (own pace, time for people) and the risks (screen time, handing thinking over, wrong answers, privacy, cost and equity, and an unstructured afternoon). Fear of job loss, the seventh risk, is left out because none of the statements concerns it.

You see eight statements, one at a time, and place each in one bin. Ask what the statement says about the effect on students. A single story often supplies one benefit and one risk: freed afternoons can mean projects with adult guides, or phones and games with no plan. Check figures against the sources.

## How to Use

1. Read the statement in the white box.
2. Click the benefit or risk you think fits. The button turns blue.
3. Press **Submit**. The box below the statement says whether you are right and why.
4. If you are wrong, the button you chose turns orange. Choose again and press **Submit**. The second try does not change the score. After a second miss, the right answer turns green.
5. Press **Next** to go on. After the last statement, press **Finish** to see your score, with each statement and its bin.
6. Press **Start over** at any time to reshuffle and begin again.
7. Check **Show definitions** to read what each benefit or risk means. Uncheck it to go back.

Only your first try on each statement counts toward the score. The statements are shuffled each run. Nothing moves until you act, and the MicroSim does not store your answers.

## Lesson Plan

### Learning Objective

After using this MicroSim, the learner can **distinguish** eight statements drawn from AI-first school stories as one of two benefits or one of six risks, correctly placing at least seven of eight on the first attempt. (Bloom level: Analyze.)

### Audience

Teachers, school administrators, school board members, education policymakers, and parents.

### Duration

10-15 minutes

### Prerequisites

- Benefits of AI-first schools, risks of AI-first schools, and the risk-and-response framework, from the section "A Framework for Reading Cases" in Chapter 22.

### Activities

1. **Read the bins (2 min).** Check **Show definitions** and read the eight bins.
2. **Distinguish (6 min).** Place all eight statements. Aim for 7 of 8 on the first try.
3. **Pair (5 min).** For each risk, name the response a school could make, as Chapter 22 does.

### Assessment

Ask learners to answer without the MicroSim, then check each answer by using it.

1. "Tuition of $40,000 to $65,000 a year limits who can attend." Which bin? *(Risk: cost and equity. Price is an equity issue when it leaves students out.)*
2. Is every complaint about AI a privacy risk? *(No. A walkout over screen hours is a screen-time risk.)*
3. Can one story show both a benefit and a risk? *(Yes. The freed afternoon is a benefit when planned and a risk when it is not.)*
