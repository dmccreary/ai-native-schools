# About This Book

## Welcome from Indigo

!!! mascot-welcome "Welcome!"
    ![Indigo the Barn Owl waving welcome](img/mascot/welcome.png){ class="mascot-admonition-img" }
    Hello, I'm Indigo. This book asks a practical question: if AI can handle much of direct instruction in a few morning hours, what should students do with the rest of the day? Every story here shows both what went well and what went wrong, along with what a school can do about the risks. Let's find the balance.

## Why This Intelligent Textbook

The length of task an AI system can complete on its own is growing on a measurable curve, and schools will feel it within a single planning cycle. Most school leaders are being asked to buy AI products, write AI policies, and redesign the school day before anyone has given them a shared vocabulary or a clear look at the evidence on both sides.

**In the United States:**

- In a 2022 RAND survey, teachers reported working about **53 hours a week**, so a teacher's time is already the scarcest resource in a school[^1]
- **Alpha School**, the best-known model of two hours of adaptive academics followed by afternoon workshops, charges about **$40,000 a year** at its original Austin campus and about **$65,000** at newer campuses, far above public per-student spending[^2]
- In a randomized trial with **900 tutors and 1,800 K-12 students**, giving human tutors an AI coach (Tutor CoPilot) raised student mastery by **4 percentage points**, and by **9 points** for students of the least experienced tutors, at about **$20 per tutor per year**[^3]
- In a randomized study in a Harvard introductory physics course, students using a carefully designed AI tutor **learned more, in less time**, than students in an instructor-led active-learning class[^4]

**Worldwide:**

- METR's task-horizon studies show the length of task leading AI models can complete growing exponentially; this book plans around a doubling **about every five months**[^5]
- A six-week after-school pilot in Edo State, Nigeria, using an AI tutor with teachers in the room produced gains of about **0.3 standard deviations**, which the World Bank team compared to nearly two years of typical learning[^6]
- In a Turkish high-school field experiment, students with open access to an AI chatbot scored about **17 percent lower** on later exams taken without AI, while a version that gave hints instead of answers removed most of that harm[^7]

The same technology helped students in one study and hurt them in another. The difference was design, and the people making those design decisions for your students are the readers of this book.

This book is built on a **learning graph of 532 interconnected concepts** organized into 14 categories, so each idea is introduced only after the ideas it depends on. Its organizing framework is the **five-level model of intelligent textbooks**, adapted from the SAE levels for self-driving cars, which gives buyers and policymakers precise terms in place of vague "AI-powered" claims. The chapters embed **interactive MicroSims**, small browser-based simulations of doubling curves, effect sizes, xAPI statements, and adoption curves, so readers can test a claim instead of taking it on faith. The whole textbook is **open source and free**, with no paywalls or access codes.

## Who This Book Is For

The book is written for K-12 teachers, principals and administrators, school board members, education policymakers, curriculum and instructional designers, education students, and parents. No programming or statistics background is assumed. The few charts and growth curves are explained from first principles, and every technical term is defined when it first appears.

## How to Use This Book

Each chapter builds on earlier ones, so reading in order is recommended. Readers who already know the background can use the learning graph to jump to a topic and check its prerequisites. The book includes:

- **23 Chapters** in five parts: the evidence for the prediction (METR, the five levels, content economics, and student privacy), the shorter academic morning, the pro-social afternoon, changing the culture of a school, and putting the model into practice
- **Interactive MicroSims** embedded in the chapters, which you can manipulate to explore a concept
- **Real and composite stories** that pair every risk with what a school can do about it, collected in the [AI-First Stories](appendices/ai-first-stories.md) appendix
- **A Glossary** of 532 terms, one for every concept in the learning graph
- **An FAQ** of 105 common questions and answers
- **A Learning Graph** showing how the 532 concepts depend on each other
- **Search**, available from any page

The [Learning Graph](learning-graph/index.md) shows how concepts connect across chapters. If you want to explore out of order, or check what a topic depends on, start there.

## About the Author

![Dan McCreary](img/dan-headshot-small.png){ width="150px" align="right" }

Dan McCreary is a semi-retired AI researcher, solution architect, and educator who has spent more than three decades helping Fortune 100 organizations reason over massive datasets. At Optum he founded the Generative AI Center of Excellence and led the team that built one of the world's largest healthcare knowledge graphs, spanning over 25 billion vertices, to unify member, provider, and patient insights. His background in knowledge representation and systems thinking underpins the learning graphs and intelligent textbook workflows used throughout this book.

He is the author of *A Five-Level Classification Framework for Intelligent Textbooks: Lessons from Autonomous Vehicle Standards* (EdArXiv, 2025)[^8], the framework at the center of this book. He is also the co-author of *Making Sense of NoSQL* (Manning Publications), the founding chair of the NoSQL Now! conference, and a frequent keynote speaker on semantic search, ontology strategy, and AI hardware. Dan has mentored students as a STEM volunteer since 2014 and now applies the same rigor to building open educational resources. The [Intelligent Textbooks Case Studies](https://dmccreary.github.io/intelligent-textbooks/case-studies/) list the textbooks he has created or co-created with other authors.

**Selected Credentials**

- MBA coursework at the University of St. Thomas
- Patent holder in semantic search and ontology management techniques
