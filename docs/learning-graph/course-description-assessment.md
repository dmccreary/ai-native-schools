## Gap Analysis

1. **Broad audience (−1).** The book serves several groups with different needs. This does not block learning graph generation, but chapter writers will need to choose a main reader. Teachers and school leaders are the natural primary audience, with board members, policymakers, and parents as secondary readers.
2. **Case-study topics (−1).** Topics such as "Unbound Academy" and "lessons from Tutor CoPilot" are specific examples. In the learning graph, they work best as examples attached to general concepts (for example, *Guardrails* or *AI That Coaches Teachers*) rather than as concepts of their own.
3. **Recall of case names (−1).** "Name the key case studies and the main lesson of each" is fine for a quiz but adds little to the learning graph.

## Improvement Suggestions

These are optional; the description is already ready to use.

1. Add one sentence naming the **primary reader** (for example, "written mainly for K-12 teachers and school leaders, with sections for board members and parents").
2. When the learning graph is generated, **treat case studies as examples** of concepts, not as concepts, unless the case is central (Alpha School and the five-level model are central).
3. **Check the stories written from memory** in the AI-First Stories appendix before chapters cite their numbers. Fourteen stories are still marked "Sources still to add."
