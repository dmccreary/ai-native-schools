## Readability

The estimated Flesch-Kincaid grade level of the definitions is 12.7. That suits the book's readers, who are teachers, school leaders, board members, policymakers, and parents.

## Recommendations

1. **Lengthen the short definitions.** These 45 definitions are under 15 words. They are correct, but they could each use one more detail that tells the term apart from its neighbors: AI Energy Use, AI in Education, Bright Spots, Budget Reallocation, Clubs and Extracurriculars, Coding Club, Collaboration, Concept Node, Content Creation Cost, Cost Barrier to AI Schools, Curiosity, Dance, Detector False Positives, Digital Textbook, Driving Question, Educational Technology, Empathy, Evidence Evaluation, Family Communication, Hands-On Learning, Human Connection, Interoperability Standards, Learning Record Store, Lesson From a Case, Matching Strategy to Concern, Music Education, Observability, Organizational Change, Pilot Goals, Print Textbook, Pro-Social Behavior, Procurement Criteria, Professional Development, Removing Obstacles, Resistance to Change, Robotics Club, School Rituals, Sense of Urgency, Shared Goals, Short-Term Wins, Sports and Athletics, Stakeholder, Storytelling, Success Measures, Vendor Level Claim.
2. **Review the terms the book uses but does not define.** *Software Is Not the Model*, *Two-to-Three-Hour Learning*, *Illustrated Story*, *Graphic Novel Scene*, and the *Elementary*, *Middle* and *High School Model* entries were written from context rather than from a definition in a chapter.
3. **Check the general-knowledge entries.** The *IMS Global* entry (now 1EdTech) and the *COPPA* entry (school authorization) draw on outside knowledge and should be checked against current sources.
