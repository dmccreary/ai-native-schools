# Chapters

This textbook is organized into 23 chapters covering 532 concepts from the learning graph.

## Chapter Overview

1. [The Building Blocks of Schools, Students, and Learning](01-building-blocks-schools-students/index.md) - This chapter introduces the core vocabulary of schools, students, teachers, and learning theory — education, curriculum, learning outcomes, the school day, direct instruction, spaced and retrieval practice, Bloom's Taxonomy, and formative assessment.
2. [Evidence, Motivation, and the Stakeholders of Schooling](02-evidence-motivation-stakeholders/index.md) - This chapter covers how educators judge what works — evidence-based practice, randomized controlled trials, effect size, and case study methods — alongside the motivational side of learning: curiosity, boredom, engagement, and productive struggle.
3. [School Structures, Equity, and the Industrial-Era Legacy](03-school-structures-equity-industrial-era/index.md) - This chapter examines the structural and historical scaffolding of the school system: the bell schedule, the Carnegie unit, seat time, age-based grade levels, and the industrial-era school model, alongside achievement and equity gaps and alternatives like charter, virtual, and microschools.
4. [Measuring AI Capability: The METR Task-Horizon Studies](04-measuring-ai-capability-metr/index.md) - This chapter introduces artificial intelligence, machine learning, and large language models, then explains how METR measures AI capability through the task-horizon studies — benchmark task suites, the 50% time horizon, and human working time as the unit of measurement.
5. [Projecting AI Growth: Doubling Curves, Limits, and Uncertainty](05-projecting-ai-growth-doubling-curves/index.md) - This chapter teaches readers to project the METR task-horizon trend forward: reading a logarithmic scale, the five-month doubling time, and the difference between linear and exponential growth.
6. [The Five-Level Model of Intelligent Textbooks](06-five-level-model-intelligent-textbooks/index.md) - This chapter presents the book's central organizing idea: the five-level model of intelligent textbooks, adapted from the SAE J3016 levels for self-driving cars.
7. [Content Economics and Open Educational Resources](07-content-economics-open-educational/index.md) - This chapter covers the economics behind falling content-creation costs: open educational resources, open licenses, the OpenBook QR Score, content commoditization, and how publisher business models are changing as AI makes level 1 and level 2 content cheap to produce.
8. [Tracking Mastery: xAPI, Learning Record Stores, and MicroSims](08-tracking-mastery-xapi-learning-record/index.md) - This chapter introduces the technical building blocks that let a textbook adapt to a student without a live AI model: xAPI, Learning Record Stores, mastery data, learning analytics, and MicroSims, together with student-controlled, portable learning data.
9. [Learning Graphs, Interoperability Standards, and Access at Scale](09-learning-graphs-interoperability/index.md) - This chapter covers learning graphs as directed acyclic graphs of concept nodes, the interoperability standards (IMS Global, IEEE) that let these pieces work across products, and the practical requirements of delivering content at scale: device access, connectivity, offline access, and a "generate once, serve many" delivery model.
10. [Student Privacy and Data Governance](10-student-privacy-data-governance/index.md) - This chapter covers the level-3 privacy inflection point: why adaptive and higher-level textbooks need detailed student data, and what that triggers — FERPA, COPPA, GDPR, data governance, and algorithmic bias auditing — illustrated by the inBloom shutdown and the PowerSchool data breach.
11. [Mastery Learning and the Case for a Compressed Morning](11-mastery-learning-case-compressed-morning/index.md) - This chapter makes the case for a shorter academic morning: adaptive and mastery learning, personalized pacing, Bloom's two-sigma problem, one-on-one tutoring, and what AI tutors do well, alongside the critical- and computational-thinking skills a compressed morning is meant to protect time for.
12. [AI Guardrails: Teaching Thinking Without Over-Reliance](12-ai-guardrails-teaching-thinking-without/index.md) - This chapter covers how an AI tutor should be designed rather than just how powerful it is: guardrails, hints instead of answers, Socratic questioning, and the risk of cognitive offloading and over-reliance, alongside the specific thinking skills (decomposition, pattern recognition, abstraction, algorithmic thinking) AI can teach well.
13. [Social Learning and Human Skills](13-social-learning-human-skills/index.md) - This chapter introduces social constructionism and constructivism as the theoretical basis for the pro-social afternoon, along with the specific human skills it targets: collaboration, teamwork, communication, empathy, creativity, and social-emotional learning.
14. [The Pro-Social Afternoon](14-pro-social-afternoon/index.md) - This chapter covers what fills the pro-social afternoon in practice: the arts, sports, clubs, maker spaces, service learning, and project-based learning, with proven models such as High Tech High, phenomenon-based learning, and El Sistema, plus the risk of an unstructured afternoon.
15. [Technology Adoption and the Diffusion of Innovation](15-technology-adoption-diffusion-innovation/index.md) - This chapter introduces the technology adoption curve: Rogers' diffusion of innovations, the five innovation attributes, adopter categories from innovators to laggards, the chasm between early adopters and the early majority, and the hype cycle.
16. [Organizational Change Models](16-organizational-change-models/index.md) - This chapter names the major organizational change models used later in the book — Kotter's eight steps, Lewin's unfreeze-change-refreeze, ADKAR, Bridges' transition model, Schein's culture model, Senge's learning organization, Fullan's educational change, and the Concerns-Based Adoption Model — along with organizational and school culture.
17. [Leading Change: Vision, Culture, and Early-Stage Concerns](17-leading-change-vision-culture/index.md) - This chapter goes deeper into the early stages of leading change: Lewin's unfreezing, Kotter's guiding coalition and shared vision, Bridges' endings and losses, Schein's levels of culture, and the first stages of ADKAR and the Concerns-Based Adoption Model.
18. [Sustaining Change: Systems Thinking and National Adoption](18-sustaining-change-systems-thinking/index.md) - This chapter completes the change models with their later, sustaining stages: Senge's systems thinking and mental models, the later stages of ADKAR and CBAM, Bridges' new beginnings, the "grammar of schooling," and Estonia's national AI Leap as a whole-system example.
19. [Microstrategies for Leading Change](19-microstrategies-leading-change/index.md) - This chapter covers the concrete, next-week moves that make change stick: teacher champions, pilot classrooms, bright spots, shrinking the first step, professional learning communities, and engaging stakeholders — including resistance, family communication, and the changing role of the teacher from lecturer to coach and mentor.
20. [Designing the AI-Native School Day](20-designing-ai-native-school-day/index.md) - This chapter covers redesigning the school day itself: the morning-afternoon model, scheduling and staffing, the changing teacher role, and grade-level design choices for a real AI-native school.
21. [Implementing the AI-Native Model Responsibly](21-implementing-ai-native-model-responsibly/index.md) - This chapter covers putting the model into practice responsibly: safe procurement, avoiding vendor lock-in, equity and access, redesigning assessment instead of policing it, and measuring whether a pilot is actually working.
22. [AI-First Case Studies I: Promise and Peril](22-ai-first-case-studies-i-promise-peril/index.md) - This chapter presents a first set of real cases that show both the promise and the peril of AI-first schools: Alpha School, the Turkish high school chatbot study, the Harvard physics AI tutor, and the risk-and-response framework used to weigh them.
23. [AI-First Case Studies II: Lessons for Practice](23-ai-first-case-studies-ii-lessons/index.md) - This chapter presents a second set of cases focused on lessons for practice: AltSchool, Summit Learning, the Los Angeles iPad rollout and "Ed" chatbot, and other cautionary and positive examples, closing with how to evaluate result claims and make a recommendation to a school board.

## How to Use This Textbook

Work through the chapters in order. Each chapter's concepts depend only on concepts introduced in earlier chapters, so completing chapters sequentially ensures every prerequisite has already been covered. The Part structure of the [course description](../course-description.md) groups these 23 chapters into five broader parts plus a closing set of case studies.

**Note:** Each chapter includes a list of concepts covered. Make sure to complete prerequisites before moving to advanced chapters.
