# References: Leading Change: Vision, Culture, and Early-Stage Concerns

1. [Kurt Lewin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurt_Lewin) - Wikipedia - Biography of the social psychologist associated with unfreeze, change, refreeze, including evidence that the three-step model took final form after his death. It helps readers use the model with appropriate caution.

2. [Force-field analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Force-field_analysis) - Wikipedia - Explains Lewin's method of listing the forces driving and restraining a change. It supports the chapter's worked example of a force field for the compressed morning.

3. [Edgar Schein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edgar_Schein) - Wikipedia - Describes Schein's three levels of culture: artifacts and behaviors, espoused values, and shared basic assumptions. It is the basis for the chapter's guidance on reading a school's culture quickly.

4. Leading Change - John P. Kotter - Harvard Business Review Press - Kotter's eight-stage process, from establishing a sense of urgency and a guiding coalition to anchoring change in culture. Its list of common leadership errors explains why many school reforms stall early.

5. Managing Transitions: Making the Most of Change (4th ed.) - William Bridges with Susan Bridges - Da Capo Lifelong Books - Bridges separates external change from the internal transition people go through, and starts every transition with an ending. His loss inventory helps leaders name what teachers are giving up.

6. [The 8-Step Process for Leading Change](https://www.kotterinc.com/methodology/8-steps/) - Kotter Inc. - Lists and explains Kotter's eight steps, from creating urgency to instituting change, and notes their later evolution into "accelerators." A compact reference for the chapter's Kotter at Lincoln example.

7. [Bridges Transition Model](https://wmbridges.com/about/what-is-transition/) - William Bridges Associates - Describes endings, the neutral zone, and new beginnings, and the difference between change and transition. It supports the chapter's section on endings and losses.

8. [The Prosci ADKAR Model](https://www.prosci.com/methodology/adkar) - Prosci - Defines the five ADKAR elements, with Awareness, Desire, and Knowledge as the early stages this chapter covers. It helps leaders diagnose why an individual teacher has not yet moved.

9. [Stages of Concern](https://www.air.org/resource/stages-concern) - American Institutes for Research - Explains the seven Stages of Concern, from unconcerned and informational to refocusing, and how to assess them. It grounds the chapter's focus on teachers' early self concerns.

10. [Innovation Configurations](https://www.air.org/resource/innovation-configurations) - American Institutes for Research - Describes how to build an Innovation Configuration map that shows ideal, acceptable, and unacceptable versions of a practice. It supports the chapter's worked example on mapping the AI-native model.
