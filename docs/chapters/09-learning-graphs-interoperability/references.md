# References: Learning Graphs, Interoperability Standards, and Access at Scale

1. [Directed acyclic graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Directed_acyclic_graph) - Wikipedia - Defines directed acyclic graphs, topological ordering and their common uses. It explains why a learning graph of prerequisites can have no loops and how a valid learning path can always be found.

2. [Interoperability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interoperability) - Wikipedia - Explains interoperability, the role of open standards and the costs of vendor lock-in. It frames why districts should require standards such as LTI and OneRoster when buying learning tools.

3. [Content delivery network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Content_delivery_network) - Wikipedia - Describes how CDNs cache static files close to users so millions can load the same page cheaply. It underpins the chapter's "generate once, serve many" argument for hosting textbooks at very low cost.

4. Learning How to Learn - Joseph D. Novak and D. Bob Gowin - Cambridge University Press - Novak developed concept maps, which show ideas as linked nodes so learners see how new concepts rest on old ones. Concept maps are the direct ancestor of the learning graph's concept nodes and dependencies.

5. Knowledge Spaces - Jean-Paul Doignon and Jean-Claude Falmagne - Springer - Doignon and Falmagne's theory models what a student knows as a set of mastered items ordered by prerequisites, the basis of the ALEKS system. It gives a mathematical foundation for choosing the next concept a learner is ready for.

6. [Learning Tools Interoperability (LTI)](https://www.1edtech.org/standards/lti) - 1EdTech - The official description of LTI, which lets a tool or textbook launch securely inside an LMS. It is the standard most districts should ask about first when adding a new learning tool.

7. [Competencies and Academic Standards Exchange (CASE)](https://www.1edtech.org/standards/case) - 1EdTech - Describes CASE, a standard for publishing academic standards and competencies in machine-readable form. It shows how concepts in a learning graph can be aligned to state standards in a shareable way.

8. [OneRoster](https://www.1edtech.org/standards/oneroster) - 1EdTech - Explains OneRoster, the standard for exchanging class rosters and grades between student information systems and learning tools. It reduces manual data entry and limits the student data copied into each vendor system.

9. [IEEE Learning Technology Standards Committee](https://sagroups.ieee.org/ltsc/) - IEEE - Home page of the IEEE committee that develops learning technology standards, including xAPI and work on adaptive instructional systems. It is the IEEE side of the interoperability standards covered in this chapter.

10. [Nearly one-in-five teens can't always finish their homework because of the digital divide](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2018/10/26/nearly-one-in-five-teens-cant-always-finish-their-homework-because-of-the-digital-divide/) - Pew Research Center - Survey data on the "homework gap" for teens without reliable home internet, broken down by income and race. It grounds the chapter's case for offline access and device planning in rural and low-income schools.
