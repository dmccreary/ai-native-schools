# References: Designing the AI-Native School Day

1. [Blended Learning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blended_learning) - Wikipedia - Explains how schools combine online learning with face-to-face teaching, including rotation and flex models. The morning-afternoon model is one form of blended learning, so this article gives readers the wider family of designs.

2. [Authentic Assessment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Authentic_assessment) - Wikipedia - Describes assessment through realistic tasks that ask students to use what they know, rather than recall it. It is the basis for the chapter's performance, portfolio and mastery-based assessment ideas, which are harder to complete with AI.

3. [Artificial Intelligence Content Detection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_intelligence_content_detection) - Wikipedia - Covers software that tries to identify AI-written text and notes it is often unreliable. It reports studies showing detectors wrongly flag non-native English writers, the evidence behind the chapter's warning about detector false positives.

4. Understanding by Design (Expanded 2nd Edition) - Grant Wiggins and Jay McTighe - ASCD - Wiggins and McTighe are credited with backward design: decide what students should understand, then the evidence that would show it, and only then plan lessons. This is the logic of redesigning assessment rather than policing AI use.

5. Blended: Using Disruptive Innovation to Improve Schools - Michael B. Horn and Heather Staker - Jossey-Bass - Horn and Staker are credited with the widely used catalog of blended-learning models, such as station rotation and flex. Their models help leaders see the scheduling, staffing and room choices behind an AI-native day.

6. [Blended Learning Models](https://www.blendedlearning.org/models/) - Clayton Christensen Institute - Short descriptions and examples of seven blended-learning models, from station rotation to enriched virtual. A quick reference when drafting a schedule that pairs a morning adaptive block with afternoon group work.

7. [GPT Detectors Are Biased Against Non-Native English Writers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.02819) - arXiv (Liang, Yuksekgonul, Mao, Wu and Zou, Stanford) - The 2023 study that found popular AI detectors often label non-native English writing as AI-generated. The authors caution against using detectors in schools, which supports the chapter's equity concerns.

8. [Guidance for Generative AI in Education and Research](https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/guidance-generative-ai-education-and-research) - UNESCO - UNESCO's first global guidance on generative AI in schools, recommending data protection, age limits and a human-centered approach to teaching. Useful for checking a school-day design against international norms.

9. [AI and Education Policy 101: Lessons from Early Adopters](https://crpe.org/research/ai/) - Center on Reinventing Public Education - Reports how early-adopter districts write AI policy, starting from a court case over a student penalized for AI use. Its principles on academic integrity and stakeholder input apply directly to this chapter.

10. [AI Literacy: A Framework to Understand, Evaluate, and Use Emerging Technology](https://digitalpromise.org/initiative/artificial-intelligence-in-education/ai-literacy/) - Digital Promise - A framework and strategies for building AI literacy for students and teachers in PK-12 settings. It helps define what teachers in their new coach and facilitator roles need to know.
