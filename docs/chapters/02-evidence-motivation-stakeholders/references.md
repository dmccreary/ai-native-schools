# References: Evidence, Motivation, and the Stakeholders of Schooling

1. [Evidence-based education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evidence-based_education) - Wikipedia - Describes the movement to base teaching decisions on rigorous research, its history, and its critics. Frames the chapter's central question of how a principal should judge a vendor's claim about what works.

2. [Randomized controlled trial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Randomized_controlled_trial) - Wikipedia - Explains how random assignment to treatment and control groups supports causal conclusions, plus common limitations. Helps readers see why an RCT carries more weight than a testimonial or a before-and-after comparison.

3. [Effect size](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Effect_size) - Wikipedia - Defines effect size measures such as Cohen's d and how they express the magnitude of a difference. Gives readers the tools to read the standardized numbers that appear in education research and product brochures.

4. Visible Learning: A Synthesis of Over 800 Meta-Analyses Relating to Achievement (2009) - John Hattie - Routledge - Hattie popularized ranking teaching influences by average effect size and the 0.40 "hinge point" for judging whether an intervention beats typical growth. His approach clarifies how educators compare effect sizes across studies.

5. Why We Do What We Do: Understanding Self-Motivation (1995) - Edward L. Deci with Richard Flaste - G. P. Putnam's Sons - Deci, co-founder of self-determination theory, explains how autonomy, competence, and relatedness sustain intrinsic motivation and how rewards can undermine it. Clarifies the chapter's discussion of curiosity and engagement.

6. [What Works Clearinghouse](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/) - Institute of Education Sciences, U.S. Department of Education - The federal clearinghouse that reviews education studies against published standards and issues practice guides and intervention reports. A first stop for checking whether a program's evidence holds up.

7. [Evidence for ESSA](https://www.evidenceforessa.org/) - Johns Hopkins University Center for Research and Reform in Education - Rates PK-12 programs against the evidence tiers in the Every Student Succeeds Act. Lets school leaders look up a product by name and see the strength of its research base.

8. [Interpreting Effect Sizes of Education Interventions](https://edworkingpapers.com/ai19-10) - Matthew A. Kraft, Annenberg Institute at Brown University - Argues that Cohen's small, medium, and large labels mislead in education and offers realistic benchmarks that account for study design, cost, and scale. Essential for judging whether a reported effect is meaningful.

9. [Why Don't Students Like School?](https://www.aft.org/ae/spring2009/willingham) - Daniel T. Willingham, American Educator (AFT) - A cognitive scientist explains that people enjoy thinking only when problems sit at the right difficulty, building on prior knowledge. Connects curiosity, boredom, and productive struggle to how the mind works.

10. [AAP Family Media Plan](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/fmp/Pages/MediaPlan.aspx) - American Academy of Pediatrics, HealthyChildren.org - An interactive tool families use to set screen-time and media rules by age. Shows parents' perspective on screen time, a concern any school adding AI-driven screen learning must address.
