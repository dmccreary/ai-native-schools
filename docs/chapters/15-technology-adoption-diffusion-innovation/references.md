# References: Technology Adoption and the Diffusion of Innovation

1. [Diffusion of innovations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diffusion_of_innovations) - Wikipedia - Covers Rogers' theory of how new ideas spread, including the five innovation attributes, adopter categories, opinion leaders, and the S-curve. It is the backbone of this chapter's vocabulary.

2. [Technology adoption life cycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technology_adoption_life_cycle) - Wikipedia - Describes the bell curve of innovators, early adopters, early majority, late majority, and laggards, and notes Moore's chasm between the first groups. It helps readers place teachers on the adoption curve.

3. [Gartner hype cycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gartner_hype_cycle) - Wikipedia - Explains the five phases from technology trigger to plateau of productivity and summarizes criticism that few technologies follow the full cycle. Useful for judging generative AI claims with appropriate skepticism.

4. Diffusion of Innovations (5th ed.) - Everett M. Rogers - Free Press - Rogers' synthesis of decades of diffusion studies, credited with the five attributes (relative advantage, compatibility, complexity, trialability, observability) and the adopter categories. It explains why a good innovation can still spread slowly.

5. Crossing the Chasm (3rd ed.) - Geoffrey A. Moore - HarperBusiness - Moore's argument that pragmatist buyers will not follow visionaries without a whole product and peer references, and that a beachhead strategy wins them. It explains why pilot success with enthusiasts rarely spreads district-wide.

6. [Diffusion of Innovations (TheoryHub)](https://open.ncl.ac.uk/theories/8/diffusion-of-innovations/) - Newcastle University - A plain-language review of Rogers' theory, covering attributes, the innovation-decision process, the S-curve, critical mass, and criticisms such as pro-innovation bias. A balanced short reference for leadership teams.

7. [Quaking Up with Geoffrey Moore](https://strategy-business.com/article/16930) - strategy+business - A free profile of Moore and his adoption frameworks, from Crossing the Chasm onward, and how they apply to established organizations. It adds context for the chasm, pragmatists, and whole-product ideas in the chapter.

8. [Top Ed-Tech Trends of 2013: MOOCs and Anti-MOOCs](https://hackeducation.com/2013/11/29/top-ed-tech-trends-2013-moocs) - Hack Education (Audrey Watters) - A critic's account of MOOCs sliding from the peak of inflated expectations into the trough of disillusionment. It is a concrete recent example of an edtech hype wave.

9. [The Recurring Cycle of Hype and Despair Around Ed Tech](https://punyamishra.com/2014/08/20/here-we-go-again-the-recurring-cycle-of-hype-and-despair-around-ed-tech/) - Punya Mishra - Traces repeated edtech promises, from 1930s talking pictures to MOOCs, and argues they fail when they focus on the tool rather than teaching. A useful caution for AI adoption plans.

10. [AI Leap (TI-Hüpe)](https://tihupe.ee/en) - AI Leap Foundation, Estonia - Describes Estonia's national program pairing teacher training, a guided AI learning app, and independent evaluation by the University of Tartu. It is the chapter's main example of a system adopting on purpose.
