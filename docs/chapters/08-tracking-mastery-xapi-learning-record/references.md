# References: Tracking Mastery: xAPI, Learning Record Stores, and MicroSims

1. [Experience API](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Experience_API) - Wikipedia - Describes xAPI, its actor-verb-object statements, its origin at the Advanced Distributed Learning Initiative and its relationship to SCORM. A concise primer for the chapter's core standard.

2. [Learning analytics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Learning_analytics) - Wikipedia - Explains how data about learners and their activity is collected, analyzed and reported to improve teaching. It frames why mastery data from MicroSims and quizzes matters, along with the ethical questions it raises.

3. [Learning management system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Learning_management_system) - Wikipedia - Covers what an LMS does, its history and common products such as Canvas and Moodle. It helps readers see how an LMS differs from a learning record store and where each fits in a school.

4. Data & Analytics for Instructional Designers - Megan Torrance - ATD Press - Torrance, a leading xAPI practitioner, shows designers how to decide what learner data to collect and how to turn xAPI statements into useful questions. Her practical approach suits non-programmers planning what a textbook should record.

5. Embedded Formative Assessment - Dylan Wiliam - Solution Tree Press - Wiliam's argument that evidence of learning is only valuable when it changes the next teaching decision. This idea explains why mastery data should drive what a student sees next rather than sit in a gradebook.

6. [What is xAPI?](https://xapi.com/overview/) - Rustici Software, xapi.com - A plain-language overview of xAPI, statements and learning record stores from a company that helped write the specification. A good first read for educators before the formal specification.

7. [What is a Learning Record Store?](https://xapi.com/learning-record-store/) - Rustici Software, xapi.com - Explains what an LRS stores, how it receives statements and how it differs from an LMS. It supports the chapter's explanation of where mastery records live and who can read them.

8. [xAPI Specification](https://github.com/adlnet/xAPI-Spec) - Advanced Distributed Learning Initiative (ADL) - The official, openly published xAPI specification, including statement structure, verbs and LRS behavior. Technical staff and vendors can use it to check whether a product really follows the standard.

9. [IEEE 9274.1.1-2023: JSON Data Model Format and RESTful Web Service for Learner Experience Data Tracking and Access](https://standards.ieee.org/ieee/9274.1.1/7321/) - IEEE Standards Association - The page for xAPI as a formal IEEE standard. It shows that xAPI is a stable, vendor-neutral standard, which matters for data portability and procurement decisions.

10. [Caliper Analytics](https://www.1edtech.org/standards/caliper) - 1EdTech - Describes Caliper, 1EdTech's alternative standard for capturing learning activity events. Comparing it with xAPI helps districts ask vendors which event standards they support and whether data can move between systems.
