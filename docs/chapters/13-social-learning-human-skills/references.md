# References: Social Learning and Human Skills

1. [Zone of proximal development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zone_of_proximal_development) - Wikipedia - Explains Vygotsky's gap between what a learner can do alone and with guidance, plus scaffolding. It grounds the chapter's case that peers and teachers, not software alone, stretch students' thinking.

2. [Constructionism (learning theory)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constructionism_(learning_theory)) - Wikipedia - Describes Seymour Papert's theory that people learn best while building shareable things. It supports the chapter's treatment of hands-on making and learning by building together in the afternoon.

3. [Social–emotional learning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_emotional_learning) - Wikipedia - Covers SEL's origins, the CASEL five competencies, research on benefits, and the political debate around it. Useful for readers who will meet both supporters and critics of SEL in their communities.

4. Mind in Society: The Development of Higher Psychological Processes - L. S. Vygotsky (edited by Michael Cole et al.) - Harvard University Press - The source of the zone of proximal development and the claim that higher thinking first appears between people before it is internalized. It explains why social constructivism needs real human partners.

5. Mindstorms: Children, Computers, and Powerful Ideas - Seymour Papert - Basic Books - Papert's founding statement of constructionism, with his image of children learning mathematics by programming a turtle. It shows how building a public artifact turns abstract ideas into something students can test and discuss.

6. [What Is the CASEL Framework?](https://casel.org/fundamentals-of-sel/what-is-the-casel-framework/) - CASEL - Defines the five SEL competencies: self-awareness, self-management, social awareness, relationship skills, and responsible decision-making. These are the categories the chapter uses for self-awareness, relationship skills, and belonging.

7. [The Jigsaw Classroom](https://www.jigsaw.org/) - Jigsaw.org (Elliot Aronson) - Explains Aronson's cooperative learning technique in which each student holds one piece of the lesson. It is a concrete, tested model for shared goals and interdependent team roles.

8. [Empathy Defined](https://greatergood.berkeley.edu/topic/empathy/definition) - Greater Good Science Center, UC Berkeley - Distinguishes affective empathy from cognitive empathy, also called perspective taking, and summarizes research on building it. It supports the chapter's worked example on three kinds of empathy.

9. [Lifelong Kindergarten](https://www.media.mit.edu/groups/lifelong-kindergarten/overview/) - MIT Media Lab - The research group behind Scratch, which designs tools and activities that help young people learn as creative, collaborative makers. It shows constructionism and creative confidence in current practice.

10. [Making Caring Common](https://mcc.gse.harvard.edu/) - Harvard Graduate School of Education - A research project with free school resources on raising children who care about others, including its Caring Schools Network. Useful for leaders designing pro-social goals and belonging into the school day.
