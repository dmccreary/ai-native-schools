# References: The Pro-Social Afternoon

1. [Project-based learning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project-based_learning) - Wikipedia - Surveys the history, design features, and research on project-based learning, including driving questions and public products. It gives background for the chapter's project and exhibition sections.

2. [Service-learning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Service-learning) - Wikipedia - Defines service-learning as a balance of learning goals and community service, and reviews benefits, critiques, and what makes programs effective. It helps leaders avoid service projects that are charity without learning.

3. [Extracurricular activity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extracurricular_activity) - Wikipedia - Summarizes research linking clubs, sports, and arts participation to lower dropout rates, better achievement, and stronger friendships. It supports the chapter's argument that structured afternoon activities build skills, not just fill time.

4. Lifelong Kindergarten: Cultivating Creativity through Projects, Passion, Peers, and Play - Mitchel Resnick - MIT Press - Resnick's "four P's" framework, drawn from his Scratch work, describes how creative learning grows. It gives a simple test for whether a maker space, coding club, or robotics team is truly creative.

5. Setting the Standard for Project Based Learning - John Larmer, John Mergendoller, and Suzie Boss - ASCD - The book behind the Gold Standard PBL model, setting out essential design elements such as a challenging problem, sustained inquiry, and a public product. It separates real projects from end-of-unit "dessert" activities.

6. [Gold Standard PBL: Essential Project Design Elements](https://www.pblworks.org/what-is-pbl/gold-standard-project-design) - PBLWorks - Lists the seven design elements of high-quality projects, from a challenging question to critique, revision, and a public product. A practical checklist for the chapter's "Is This a Good Project?" worked example.

7. [Phenomena](https://www.nextgenscience.org/resources/phenomena) - Next Generation Science Standards - Explains how observable events anchor science instruction and links to a guide for choosing phenomena. It supports the chapter's phenomenon-based learning section, where one real event crosses several subjects.

8. [Service-Learning K-12 Standards](https://www.nylc.org/page/standards) - National Youth Leadership Council - Presents eight standards for quality service-learning, including meaningful service, reflection, youth voice, and partnerships. A ready framework for planning service cycles and community partnership agreements.

9. [Project Play](https://projectplay.org/) - Aspen Institute - Research and guidance on youth sports access and quality, including school sports and fair play. It helps leaders design athletics that build teamwork for every student, not only the most skilled.

10. [Massachusetts Afterschool Research Study (MARS)](https://niost.wcwonline.org/Completed-Projects/researching-quality-afterschool-programming-massachusetts) - National Institute on Out-of-School Time, Wellesley Centers for Women - A study of which afterschool quality features and participation levels lead to positive youth outcomes. It informs the chapter's distinction between a structured afternoon and drift.
