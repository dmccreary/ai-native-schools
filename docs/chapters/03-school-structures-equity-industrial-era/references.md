# References: School Structures, Equity, and the Industrial-Era Legacy

1. [Carnegie Unit and Student Hour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnegie_Unit_and_Student_Hour) - Wikipedia - Traces how the Carnegie unit made 120 hours of class time the standard measure of a high school credit. Explains the origin of seat time, a structure that mastery-based models must work around.

2. [Achievement gaps in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achievement_gaps_in_the_United_States) - Wikipedia - Reviews documented gaps in test scores and attainment by race, income, and other factors, and proposed causes. Provides the background for the chapter's discussion of educational equity and learning gains.

3. [Microschool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microschool) - Wikipedia - Describes very small schools, often mixed-age and personalized, and their growth as an alternative to conventional schools. Useful context for the alternative models the chapter compares, including AI-first private schools.

4. Tinkering Toward Utopia: A Century of Public School Reform (1995) - David Tyack and Larry Cuban - Harvard University Press - Tyack and Cuban coined the "grammar of schooling," the bells, grades, and age cohorts that resist reform. Their idea explains why industrial-era structures persist and why new models keep reverting to old patterns.

5. Teachers and Machines: The Classroom Use of Technology Since 1920 (1986) - Larry Cuban - Teachers College Press - Cuban documented the repeated cycle of hype, limited adoption, and disappointment for film, radio, and television in classrooms. His pattern is the lens the chapter uses for reading the history of educational technology.

6. [The Carnegie Unit: A Century-Old Standard in a Changing Education Landscape](https://www.carnegiefoundation.org/resources/publications/carnegie-unit/) - Carnegie Foundation for the Advancement of Teaching - A 2015 report from the unit's own creator assessing whether time-based credit still serves students. Shows that even its originator calls seat time a crude proxy for learning.

7. [The Nation's Report Card (NAEP)](https://www.nationsreportcard.gov/) - National Center for Education Statistics - National and state results from the National Assessment of Educational Progress, with tools to compare student groups. The standard independent source for measuring achievement gaps and learning gains over time.

8. [Public Charter School Enrollment](https://nces.ed.gov/programs/coe/indicator/cgb) - NCES Condition of Education - Federal data on how charter enrollment has grown, which states allow charters, and the share serving high-poverty communities. Puts numbers behind the chapter's survey of alternatives to traditional schools.

9. [Center for Research on Education Outcomes (CREDO)](https://credo.stanford.edu/) - Stanford University - Publishes the National Charter School Study comparing charter and traditional public school student growth. An example of independent evaluation, contrasted in this chapter with results that schools report about themselves.

10. [Teachers' job stress has improved to pre-pandemic levels. They're still pretty stressed out.](https://chalkbeat.org/2024/06/18/rand-survey-finds-teachers-have-high-stress-long-hours-low-pay) - Chalkbeat - Reports RAND's 2024 State of the American Teacher survey on burnout, 53-hour work weeks, and pay. Documents the teacher workload and burnout problem that AI-native school designs claim to relieve.
