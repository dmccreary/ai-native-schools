# References: Mastery Learning and the Case for a Compressed Morning

1. [Mastery learning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mastery_learning) - Wikipedia - Describes mastery learning, its roots in Carroll and Bloom, and research on its effects and costs. It is the starting point for the chapter's claim that students should advance by understanding, not by seat time.

2. [Bloom's 2 sigma problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloom%27s_2_sigma_problem) - Wikipedia - Summarizes Bloom's 1984 finding that tutored students outperformed classroom students by about two standard deviations, plus later critiques of that size. It helps readers weigh the evidence behind the compressed morning.

3. [Adaptive learning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adaptive_learning) - Wikipedia - Explains how adaptive systems adjust content and pacing to each learner, with history and examples. It connects personalized pacing and gap filling to the technology that makes them practical for a whole class.

4. Human Characteristics and School Learning - Benjamin S. Bloom - McGraw-Hill - Bloom's full theory of mastery learning argues that most students can master material when given enough time and corrective feedback. It is the foundation for the chapter's argument that time, not ability, is often the real variable.

5. Brave New Words: How AI Will Revolutionize Education (and Why That's a Good Thing) - Salman Khan - Viking - Khan describes building Khanmigo, an AI tutor designed to guide rather than give answers, as a route to Bloom's two-sigma tutoring at scale. He also discusses cheating and misuse, which helps balance the case.

6. [The Impressive Effects of Tutoring on PreK-12 Learning](https://www.nber.org/papers/w27476) - Nickow, Oreopoulos and Quan, National Bureau of Economic Research - A meta-analysis of tutoring experiments finding an average effect of about 0.37 standard deviations, strongest with trained tutors and in early grades. A more realistic benchmark than Bloom's two sigma.

7. [AI tutoring outperforms in-class active learning](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-97652-6) - Kestin et al., Scientific Reports - A Harvard physics randomized trial in which a carefully designed AI tutor produced larger learning gains in less time than active-learning class sessions. Strong evidence for the benefit side, limited to one course and setting.

8. [From chalkboards to chatbots: Transforming learning in Nigeria](https://blogs.worldbank.org/en/education/From-chalkboards-to-chatbots-Transforming-learning-in-Nigeria) - World Bank - Reports a six-week, teacher-supported AI tutoring pilot in Nigeria with gains of about 0.3 standard deviations in English. It also lists caveats such as attendance problems and unpublished results.

9. [National Student Support Accelerator](https://studentsupportaccelerator.org/) - Stanford University - Research and toolkits on high-impact human tutoring, including dosage, group size and scheduling. It helps schools compare AI tutoring with proven human tutoring and plan how the two can work together.

10. [Khanmigo](https://www.khanmigo.ai/) - Khan Academy - The product page for Khan Academy's AI tutor and teacher assistant, which is designed to prompt students rather than hand them answers. It shows how one widely used AI tutor is built and marketed to schools.
