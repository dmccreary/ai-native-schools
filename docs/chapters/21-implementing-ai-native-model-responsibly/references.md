# References: Implementing the AI-Native Model Responsibly

1. [Vendor Lock-In](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vendor_lock-in) - Wikipedia - Explains how customers become tied to one supplier because switching is too costly, with real examples. It names the risk the chapter's contract checks are designed to prevent, such as data export and exit terms.

2. [Digital Divide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_divide) - Wikipedia - Describes unequal access to and use of digital technology across income, place, race, age and disability. It frames the chapter's question of whether an AI-native model closes or widens achievement gaps.

3. [Program Evaluation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Program_evaluation) - Wikipedia - Covers how to collect and analyze evidence about whether a program meets its goals and gives value for money. Background for the chapter's success measures, comparison groups and decisions about whether to scale a pilot.

4. Learning to Improve: How America's Schools Can Get Better at Getting Better - Anthony S. Bryk, Louis M. Gomez, Alicia Grunow and Paul G. LeMahieu - Harvard Education Press - The authors are credited with bringing improvement science and Plan-Do-Study-Act cycles to schools. Their "learn fast, fail fast, improve quickly" approach shows how to run a pilot that learns before it scales.

5. The Checklist Manifesto: How to Get Things Right - Atul Gawande - Metropolitan Books - Gawande is credited with showing that short checklists prevent errors by experts in complex work. His argument supports the chapter's use of due-diligence checklists for contracts, pilots and procurement decisions.

6. [Carnegie Improvement Science Collection](https://www.carnegiefoundation.org/our-work/improvement-science/) - Carnegie Foundation for the Advancement of Teaching - A free library of improvement science tools, including PDSA cycle templates and the 90-Day Cycle Handbook. Practical support for pilot governance and evaluation.

7. [What Works Clearinghouse](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/) - Institute of Education Sciences - The federal source for reviews of education research, practice guides and intervention reports. Useful when a vendor makes claims about results, since it shows which kinds of studies count as strong evidence.

8. [Empowering Education Leaders: A Toolkit for Safe, Ethical, and Equitable AI Integration](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED661924) - U.S. Department of Education, Office of Educational Technology (via ERIC) - The October 2024 toolkit on privacy, bias, procurement and planning for AI in schools. Full text is free, and its risk modules match the chapter's due-diligence steps.

9. [Common Sense Privacy Program](https://privacy.commonsense.org/) - Common Sense Media - Publishes evaluations of the privacy policies of education technology products and a set of privacy questions for educators. A practical starting point for checking a vendor's data practices before signing a contract.

10. [What Went Wrong with L.A. Unified's iPad Program?](https://www.govtech.com/education/what-went-wrong-with-la-unifieds-ipad-program.html) - Government Technology - A 2015 account of the Los Angeles iPad rollout: weak planning, thin teacher training, unfinished curriculum and dependence on one vendor. A clear warning about rushed procurement and scaling before piloting.
