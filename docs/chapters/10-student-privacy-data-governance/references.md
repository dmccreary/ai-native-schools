# References: Student Privacy and Data Governance

1. [Family Educational Rights and Privacy Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Family_Educational_Rights_and_Privacy_Act) - Wikipedia - Summarizes FERPA, the rights it gives parents and eligible students, the school-official exception and key court cases. Essential background for any district contract that shares education records with a vendor.

2. [Children's Online Privacy Protection Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Children%27s_Online_Privacy_Protection_Act) - Wikipedia - Explains COPPA, its parental consent rules for children under 13, enforcement history and later amendments. It clarifies what online services, including AI tutors, must do before collecting data from young students.

3. [General Data Protection Regulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Data_Protection_Regulation) - Wikipedia - Covers the EU's GDPR, including data minimization, lawful basis, the right to erasure and large fines. It shows a stricter privacy model that U.S. schools can borrow from when writing data policies.

4. Understanding Privacy - Daniel J. Solove - Harvard University Press - Solove replaces a single definition of privacy with a taxonomy of harms: collection, processing, dissemination and invasion. This helps school leaders see that aggregating a detailed learning history can cause harm even when no single record is secret.

5. Weapons of Math Destruction: How Big Data Increases Inequality and Threatens Democracy - Cathy O'Neil - Crown - O'Neil shows how opaque, large-scale, self-reinforcing models harm people, including teacher-evaluation algorithms. Her three tests (opacity, scale, damage) give a practical lens for algorithmic bias auditing and transparency in schools.

6. [FERPA](https://studentprivacy.ed.gov/ferpa) - U.S. Department of Education, Student Privacy Policy Office - The official text and guidance for FERPA, with links to FAQs and training. It is the authoritative source when a district needs to know what it may share with an AI vendor.

7. [Complying with COPPA: Frequently Asked Questions](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/complying-coppa-frequently-asked-questions) - Federal Trade Commission - The FTC's detailed COPPA guidance, including a section on when schools may consent on behalf of parents. It answers the practical consent questions raised by AI tools used with elementary students.

8. [What is GDPR, the EU's new data protection law?](https://gdpr.eu/what-is-gdpr/) - GDPR.eu - A plain-language summary of GDPR's principles, rights and penalties. It helps readers compare U.S. student privacy law with a broader rights-based model, especially on data minimization and retention.

9. [Education Privacy](https://fpf.org/issue/education/) - Future of Privacy Forum - FPF's education privacy hub (formerly Student Privacy Compass), with guides, state law trackers and resources for parents and schools. It offers balanced, practical help for building a district data governance program.

10. [K12 Security Information eXchange](https://www.k12six.org/) - K12 SIX - A nonprofit that tracks cyber incidents in U.S. schools and publishes security guidance for districts. It provides context for breaches like PowerSchool and practical steps for managing vendor risk and cybersecurity.
