# References: Measuring AI Capability: The METR Task-Horizon Studies

1. [Artificial intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_intelligence) - Wikipedia - A broad overview of AI's goals, methods, history, and risks. Gives readers the top rung of the chapter's ladder of terms, from artificial intelligence down to machine learning, language models, and agents.

2. [Machine learning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machine_learning) - Wikipedia - Explains how systems learn patterns from data instead of following hand-written rules, with the main families of methods. Clarifies the step between AI in general and the large language models the chapter measures.

3. [Large language model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Large_language_model) - Wikipedia - Covers how large language models are trained on text, how they generate responses, and their known limitations. Background for understanding the chatbots and AI agents whose task horizons METR tracks.

4. Artificial Intelligence: A Guide for Thinking Humans (2019) - Melanie Mitchell - Farrar, Straus and Giroux - Mitchell is known for explaining why benchmark scores can overstate what AI understands. Her careful treatment of benchmarks helps readers see why METR measured capability in human working time instead of test scores.

5. Co-Intelligence: Living and Working with AI (2024) - Ethan Mollick - Portfolio - Mollick introduced the "jagged frontier," the idea that AI is strong at some tasks and weak at others of similar difficulty. The image explains why a single task-horizon number needs careful interpretation.

6. [Measuring AI Ability to Complete Long Tasks](https://metr.org/blog/2025-03-19-measuring-ai-ability-to-complete-long-tasks/) - METR - METR's plain-language summary of the task-horizon study, showing how a logistic curve finds the task length where a model succeeds half the time. The primary source for this chapter's central measurement.

7. [Measuring AI Ability to Complete Long Software Tasks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.14499) - Kwa, West, Becker, et al., arXiv - The full research paper behind the METR study, detailing the benchmark task suites, human baseline timings, and limitations. For readers who want the methods and caveats behind the 50 percent time horizon.

8. [Task-Completion Time Horizons of Frontier AI Models](https://metr.org/time-horizons/) - METR - METR's reference page listing 50 percent and 80 percent time horizons for frontier models, with an FAQ on what the measure does and does not mean. Helps readers avoid common misreadings of the task-horizon figure.

9. [Trends in AI](https://epoch.ai/trends) - Epoch AI - A dashboard tracking growth in training compute, hardware performance, costs, and data centers, each with doubling times. Places the METR trend alongside the other exponential measurements that drive AI capability.

10. [Elements of AI](https://www.elementsofai.com/) - University of Helsinki and MinnaLearn - A free, self-paced introduction to what AI is and what it can and cannot do, requiring no math or programming. A good starting point for educators new to the chapter's terms.
