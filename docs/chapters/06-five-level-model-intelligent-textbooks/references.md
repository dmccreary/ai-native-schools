# References: The Five-Level Model of Intelligent Textbooks

1. [Textbook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Textbook) - Wikipedia - Covers the history, economics, and formats of textbooks, from print to digital and open textbooks. Background for the chapter's starting point, the print and PDF textbooks that sit at Level 1.

2. [Self-driving car](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-driving_car) - Wikipedia - Describes autonomous vehicles and the SAE J3016 levels of driving automation, from no automation to full automation. Shows the model the five-level textbook framework borrows its structure from.

3. [Intelligent tutoring system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intelligent_tutoring_system) - Wikipedia - Explains computer tutors that model student knowledge and adapt instruction, with their history and research results. Context for the adaptive Level 3 and conversational Level 4 textbooks.

4. Brave New Words: How AI Will Revolutionize Education (and Why That's a Good Thing) (2024) - Salman Khan - Viking - Khan describes the design of Khanmigo, a chatbot tutor that asks guiding questions instead of giving answers. His account illustrates conversational tutoring and the promise and limits of Level 4 textbooks.

5. Building Intelligent Interactive Tutors: Student-Centered Strategies for Revolutionizing E-Learning (2009) - Beverly Park Woolf - Morgan Kaufmann - Woolf explains how tutors combine a student model, a domain model, and a teaching model to adapt instruction. Her framework clarifies what makes a Level 3 adaptive textbook different from Level 2.

6. [A Five-Level Classification Framework for Intelligent Textbooks: Lessons from Autonomous Vehicle Standards](https://doi.org/10.35542/osf.io/sh2yu_v1) - Dan McCreary, EdArXiv - The December 2025 preprint that adapts SAE J3016 to define five textbook levels for procurement and level-specific policy. The primary source for this chapter's framework.

7. [The Five Levels of Intelligent Textbooks](https://dmccreary.github.io/intelligent-textbooks/concepts/five-levels-of-intelligent-textbooks) - Intelligent Textbooks site - A short web summary of each level, from static to autonomous, noting that Level 3 is where student data tracking begins. Useful as a quick reference when classifying a product.

8. [Khanmigo](https://www.khanmigo.ai/) - Khan Academy - The product page for Khan Academy's AI tutor and teaching assistant, which guides students toward answers. A real-world Level 4 example readers can examine when practicing level classification.

9. [ASSISTments](https://www.assistments.org/) - ASSISTments - A free nonprofit math practice platform that gives students instant feedback and hints and gives teachers real-time reports. A concrete example for testing where an interactive or adaptive product falls on the five-level scale.

10. [OpenStax](https://openstax.org/) - Rice University - Free, openly licensed, peer-reviewed textbooks available online and as PDFs. A widely used example of digital textbooks at Levels 1 and 2, useful as a baseline when comparing products.
