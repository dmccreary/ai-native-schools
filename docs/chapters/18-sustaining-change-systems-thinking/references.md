# References: Sustaining Change: Systems Thinking and National Adoption

1. [Systems thinking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Systems_thinking) - Wikipedia - Introduces systems thinking as a focus on wholes, relationships, and feedback, with roots in the work of Forrester, Meadows, and Senge. It frames the chapter's view of a school as a system.

2. [Causal loop diagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Causal_loop_diagram) - Wikipedia - Explains how variables, links, and reinforcing or balancing feedback loops are drawn and read. It supports the chapter's worked example mapping two loops in an AI rollout.

3. [Mental model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mental_model) - Wikipedia - Describes mental models as internal pictures of how the world works and connects them to single- and double-loop learning. It helps explain why teachers' beliefs about school can block a reform.

4. Thinking in Systems: A Primer - Donella H. Meadows (edited by Diana Wright) - Chelsea Green Publishing - Meadows' primer on stocks, flows, feedback loops, and leverage points. Her plain examples show why pushing harder on one part of a system often produces resistance.

5. Tinkering Toward Utopia: A Century of Public School Reform - David Tyack and Larry Cuban - Harvard University Press - Tyack and Cuban coined the "grammar of schooling," the lasting structures such as grade levels and fixed periods. It explains why reforms are often absorbed without real change.

6. [Leverage Points: Places to Intervene in a System](https://donellameadows.org/archives/leverage-points-places-to-intervene-in-a-system/) - The Donella Meadows Project - Meadows' essay ranking twelve places to intervene in a system, from parameters up to goals and paradigms. It helps leaders choose changes that last rather than surface fixes.

7. [Causal Loop Construction: The Basics](https://thesystemsthinker.com/causal-loop-construction-the-basics/) - The Systems Thinker - A step-by-step guide to choosing variables, labeling links, and identifying reinforcing and balancing loops. A practical companion for drawing the chapter's causal loop diagrams.

8. [Using "Fixes That Fail" to Get Off the Problem-Solving Treadmill](https://thesystemsthinker.com/using-fixes-that-fail-to-get-off-the-problem-solving-treadmill/) - The Systems Thinker - Daniel Kim explains how quick fixes create long-term problems and offers steps to find higher-leverage action. It sheds light on change fatigue and reform without real change.

9. [Levels of Use](https://www.air.org/resource/levels-use-concerns-based-adoption-model) - American Institutes for Research - Describes the eight Levels of Use, from nonuse through routine and refined use to renewal. It supports the chapter's completion of CBAM and its worked example reading three teachers.

10. [National Assembly Passes Bill to Switch AI-Driven Textbooks into 'Education Materials'](https://www.koreatimes.co.kr/southkorea/society/20250804/national-assembly-passes-bill-to-switch-ai-driven-textbooks-into-education-materials) - The Korea Times - Reports South Korea's August 2025 law downgrading AI digital textbooks to optional materials after concerns about teacher resistance, screen time, and literacy. It is the chapter's contrast case to Estonia.
