    Quiz: The Pro-Social Afternoon¶

    Test your understanding of what fills the pro-social afternoon, how to keep it structured, and how to design activities that build real human skills with these review questions.

    1. What do the letters in the SAFE practices for afternoon and after-school programs stand for?¶

    1. Supervised, Assessed, Fun, Enriching
    2. Sequenced, Active, Focused, Explicit
    3. Social, Artistic, Fitness, Exhibition
    4. Structured, Accountable, Funded, Evaluated
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. The 2010 meta-analysis by Durlak, Weissberg, and Pachan found that after-school programs using four practices improved personal and social skills, school bonding, and achievement, while programs without them showed no such gains. The practices are Sequenced (a connected set of activities), Active (students practice the skills), Focused (at least one component on personal or social skills), and Explicit (specific, stated goals). The chapter warns that "supervised" is not the same as structured.

    2. According to the chapter, which four features distinguish a pro-social activity from one that merely happens in a group?¶

    1. It is free, voluntary, held outdoors, and led by older students
    2. It uses technology, earns credit, has a grade, and has a teacher
    3. It is competitive, timed, scored, and open to every grade level
    4. It needs others, has a shared goal, real stakes, and a reflection
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. A pro-social activity cannot be done alone, has a shared goal, has a real audience or real stakes, and includes a short reflection on how the group worked. A soccer scrimmage fits, while a room of students silently doing separate worksheets does not, even though both involve a group. The chapter notes that the reflection feature often depends on an adult who makes the group stop and ask how it worked.

    3. A chess club has a term plan that builds from openings to a tournament, students play every session, and goals for the chess skills are posted on the wall. There is no part of the program aimed at personal or social skills. How does it rate on the SAFE scale?¶

    1. Structured, since it has a sequence and active play
    2. Unstructured, since chess is an individual game
    3. Partly structured, since it meets three of the four practices
    4. Structured, since posted goals make up for the missing practice
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. The club is Sequenced (openings to tournament), Active (students play), and Explicit (posted goals), but not Focused, because no component develops personal or social skills. The chapter's scale rates four of four as structured, two or three as partly structured, and zero or one as unstructured. A three-minute team debrief would add the missing practice. Chess being a one-on-one game does not by itself make the program unstructured.

    4. Why does the chapter call unstructured afternoon drift the main risk of the whole AI-native model?¶

    1. Because unplanned freed time turns teaching into an afternoon of babysitting
    2. Because unstructured time is more expensive than any structured program
    3. Because state laws ban unsupervised free time during the regular school day
    4. Because students in drifting afternoons spend more time on adaptive software
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. Drift is the slide of the afternoon into unplanned free time, passive supervision, or screens, where adults watch students and nothing is taught. A school that frees the time and does not plan it has replaced teaching with babysitting, keeping the cost of the compressed morning without its purpose. The chapter's gym example costs nothing, so drift is not about expense, and the room was supervised, which shows supervision alone is not enough.

    5. A teacher is planning an afternoon project on nutrition. Which of these is a driving question by the chapter's definition?¶

    1. "What are the five food groups, and which foods belong in each one?"
    2. "How could our school garden supply a monthly salad lunch for our grade?"
    3. "Which vitamins are found in leafy green vegetables?"
    4. "When was the first national school lunch program started, and by whom?"
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. A driving question is open-ended and engaging and cannot be answered by looking it up. It requires collecting data, making choices, and producing something, which also makes it harder to hand to an AI tool. The chapter's test is that if a search engine or chatbot can answer it in one reply, it is a lookup. The other three options are lookups with a single factual answer.

    See: Worked Example: Turning Topics into Driving Questions

    6. What does the chapter say the research shows about music training and academic results?¶

    1. Music training reliably raises math test scores by about half a standard deviation
    2. Music training improves grades only when it begins before a child turns five
    3. Music training has been proven to harm achievement by taking time from academics
    4. A 2020 meta-analysis found no convincing evidence it improves grades or cognition
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. Sala and Gobet's 2020 meta-analysis found no convincing evidence that music training improves broader cognitive abilities or school grades. The chapter says the arts are not a trick for raising test scores; their value lies in being forms of human expression valuable in themselves, with benefits for engagement, belonging, and cooperation, especially in group forms like ensembles. The chapter warns against over-claiming for the arts, which the first option does, and makes no claim of harm.

    7. A middle school's afternoon offers only coding club, robotics, and a maker space. Using the chapter's activity-skill matrix, which addition would best fill a gap in the skills students build?¶

    1. Service learning, such as tutoring younger students, to build empathy
    2. A second robotics team, so that more students can compete in tournaments
    3. An extra coding session focused on advanced programming languages
    4. A 3D printing and laser-cutting course added to the maker space
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. In the matrix, coding, robotics, and maker spaces all have computational thinking as their primary skill. A school offering only these graduates students strong in one cell. Service learning and mentoring have empathy as their primary skill, which is missing here. The other three options add more of the same skill. Activity-skill matching means choosing activities deliberately so each student's week covers collaboration, communication, empathy, creativity, and computational thinking.

    See: Matching Activities to Skills

    8. How does service learning differ from ordinary volunteering?¶

    1. Service learning is done only during summer break or school holidays
    2. Service learning must always take place outside the school building
    3. Service learning ties service to learning goals, with structured reflection
    4. Service learning is paid work arranged through a community partner
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. Service learning connects academic learning to a real community need, with structured reflection. Students tutoring second graders in reading, for example, first learn active listening, keep a log, and then discuss what they noticed about how the child learned. Volunteering without the learning goals and reflection is not service learning. The chapter's reading-buddies example takes place at a partner school during the school year, and nothing makes it paid work.

    9. A school forms a partnership with a downtown science museum. The program is excellent, but only a dozen students who can get rides take part. What does the chapter's analysis of community partnerships suggest?¶

    1. The partnership is a success, since the participating students learn a great deal
    2. The school should end the partnership, since museums are not part of the school day
    3. The museum should take over all afternoon programming to reach more students
    4. It has become an equity problem; the agreement should address access
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. The chapter warns that partners cluster in better-off neighborhoods and that a partnership that is a good experience for a dozen students but out of reach for the rest has become an equity problem. Its partnership checklist includes an equity line: how every student will have access, including those without transport. Safety, data, and equity are the lines most often forgotten. Ending the partnership loses real benefits, while ignoring access misses the risk.

    10. An intramural basketball coach plays only the strongest players and posts individual scoring rankings. What does the chapter's reasoning say this teaches, and what would make the program pro-social?¶

    1. It teaches discipline; adding more practice time would make it pro-social
    2. It teaches that worth depends on skill; shared playing time and debriefs would help
    3. It teaches healthy competition; no changes are needed for an intramural program
    4. It teaches teamwork; posting team rankings as well would complete the design
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. The chapter says a coach who plays only the best players teaches students that their worth depends on their skill. Its fair-play rules include giving every player at least half of each game, re-drawing teams every few weeks, training coaches to give a teamwork note, holding a three-minute debrief, and showing standings for teams rather than individual players to avoid shaming. More practice does not fix exclusion, and team sports are pro-social only when coached that way.
