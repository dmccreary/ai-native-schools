    Quiz: Technology Adoption and the Diffusion of Innovation¶

    Test your understanding of how innovations spread, the five innovation attributes, adopter categories, the chasm, and the hype cycle with these review questions.

    1. In Rogers' adopter categories, what share of adopters do innovators and early adopters make up together?¶

    1. About 2.5 percent
    2. About 34 percent
    3. About 16 percent
    4. About 50 percent
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. Innovators are 2.5 percent and early adopters 13.5 percent, for a combined 16 percent. The early majority and late majority are 34 percent each, and laggards are 16 percent. The cumulative shares at the end of each category are 2.5, 16, 50, 84, and 100 percent. The 16 percent point matters because Moore's chasm lies right after it, where many innovations that thrive with the first adopters stall.

    See: Adopter Categories and the Adoption Curve

    2. Which of Rogers' five innovation attributes is the degree to which an innovation can be tried on a limited basis before committing?¶

    1. Trialability
    2. Observability
    3. Compatibility
    4. Relative advantage
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. Trialability is the degree to which an innovation can be tried on a limited basis, such as a free Level 2 textbook used with one class for one unit. A three-year district contract has low trialability. Observability is whether results are visible to others, compatibility is fit with existing values and practice, and relative advantage is whether it is perceived as better than what it replaces.

    See: The Five Innovation Attributes

    3. Why do many innovations stall at the chasm between early adopters and the early majority?¶

    1. Because early adopters lose interest once a product becomes popular with others
    2. Because the early majority cannot afford the products that early adopters can buy
    3. Because laggards actively campaign against new products at that point
    4. Because pragmatists want peer proof and a whole product, which visionaries can't give
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. Geoffrey Moore argued that early adopters, the visionaries, accept incomplete and risky products for a chance at a big payoff. The early majority are pragmatist buyers who want a solid, proven product that fits how they already work, and they take their cue from other pragmatists. Because each group wants something different, the usual ways of winning visionaries do not work on pragmatists. Cost and laggard opposition are not the mechanism Moore describes.

    Concept Tested: The Chasm

    See: The Chasm

    4. A teacher explains why she has not tried a new adaptive tool: "The vendor requires two full days of training before I can even open it." Which innovation attribute is the barrier?¶

    1. Observability
    2. Complexity
    3. Relative advantage
    4. Compatibility
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. Complexity is the degree to which an innovation is perceived as difficult to understand and use, and it works against adoption. The chapter gives exactly this case: a teacher who must attend a two-day training before opening the software has met high complexity. Observability would be "I've never seen it used," relative advantage would be "I'm not sure it's better," and compatibility would be "it doesn't fit our pacing guide."

    See: Worked Example: A Barrier Diagnosis

    5. A principal needs a teacher to present pilot results so that cautious colleagues will try a new tool. Who is the better messenger, according to the chapter?¶

    1. The teacher who tries every new tool first and presents at national conferences
    2. The district technology director, who has the most technical knowledge
    3. A respected, cautious veteran who adopted only after seeing it work
    4. The vendor's representative, who knows the product's features best
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. An opinion leader influences through informal respect, not authority, and is often close to the group's norm. The early majority want proof from people like them, so a respected skeptic who changed her mind is the right messenger. The chapter calls the enthusiast the tempting choice and a poor messenger, because colleagues think "he'll try anything; it won't work for the rest of us." Technical knowledge and a vendor's pitch count for less than a trusted colleague's word.

    Concept Tested: Opinion Leader

    See: Worked Example: Choosing a Messenger

    6. According to the chapter, how should a school treat the trough of disillusionment in the hype cycle?¶

    1. As a time to learn with small pilots about what the technology is good for
    2. As proof that the technology has failed and should be abandoned by schools
    3. As a signal to buy at scale while vendors are offering low prices
    4. As a stage that only affects technologies with no real classroom benefits
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. The trough is the moment when claims meet evidence. Some technologies fall out of use there, and others, like MOOCs, find their real job. The chapter says the schools that gain most neither join the peak nor flee the trough; they learn during it with small pilots so they are ready when the slope of enlightenment arrives. Abandoning the technology confuses disappointment with a verdict, and buying at scale ignores what the trough teaches.

    7. A district has 600 teachers. Using Rogers' percentages, how many teachers are in the early majority?¶

    1. About 81
    2. About 300
    3. About 96
    4. About 204
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. The early majority is 34 percent of adopters, and 0.34 x 600 = 204 teachers. About 81 would be the 13.5 percent of early adopters, about 96 would be the 16 percent of innovators and early adopters combined (or laggards), and 300 is the cumulative count at the 50 percent point, the end of the early majority. The chapter's 400-teacher example shows the early majority is a different audience that needs proof, simplicity, and trusted colleagues.

    8. How does the chapter distinguish an opinion leader from a change agent?¶

    1. An opinion leader is paid by a vendor; a change agent works for the school board
    2. An opinion leader persuades by respect; a change agent works for an organization
    3. An opinion leader always adopts first; a change agent always adopts last
    4. An opinion leader sets district policy; a change agent trains new teachers
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. An opinion leader is a member of a social system whom others look to for advice, influencing through respect rather than authority. A change agent, such as an instructional coach, technology integrator, or principal, works on behalf of an organization to influence adoption in a desired direction. The chapter says the most effective change agents work through opinion leaders. Opinion leaders are not necessarily the most innovative, so they do not always adopt first.

    9. After a successful six-teacher pilot of a Level 2 textbook in seventh-grade math, a superintendent proposes rolling it out to every school and every subject next fall. Which plan best fits the chapter's analysis?¶

    1. Pick a beachhead, such as one grade in two more schools, and build the whole product
    2. Proceed with the full rollout, since the pilot proves the district is ready
    3. Wait until every teacher in the district volunteers before expanding
    4. Expand to every subject but only in the pilot school, so its teachers can lead
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. The pilot was run by innovators and early adopters, so its success does not prove the early majority is ready. Moore's beachhead strategy concentrates effort on one narrow group and makes the approach work completely before expanding. Pragmatists also need a whole product: single sign-on, short training, a parent letter, an accessibility check, and a plan for students without devices. A full rollout is crossing the chasm by hope, and waiting for every volunteer ignores the late majority and laggards.

    10. A district scores a Level 2 open textbook at 21 of 25 on the five innovation attributes and a Level 4 chat tutor at 13, mainly because the tutor is low on compatibility, complexity, and trialability. What does this comparison predict?¶

    1. The chat tutor will spread faster, since its relative advantage is the highest
    2. Both will spread at the same rate, since the attributes do not affect adoption
    3. The textbook should spread quickly; the tutor slowly, with more groundwork
    4. The textbook will stall at the chasm, since free products lack a whole product
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. Rogers found the five attributes account for roughly half or more of the variation in adoption rates. The chapter's illustrative scores predict that the Level 2 textbook, which fits existing lessons and is free to try, should spread quickly, while the Level 4 tutor, which raises privacy and cheating concerns and needs a privacy review before trial, should spread slowly. The scores are judgments, but they force a school to say why it expects spread or stall. The tutor's relative advantage was scored lower, not higher.
