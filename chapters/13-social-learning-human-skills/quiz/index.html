    Quiz: Social Learning and Human Skills¶

    Test your understanding of the learning theories behind the pro-social afternoon and the human skills it builds with these review questions.

    1. What is Vygotsky's zone of proximal development?¶

    1. The range of tasks a learner cannot yet do alone but can do with guidance
    2. The set of tasks a learner can already complete independently without any help
    3. The range of tasks too hard for a learner to complete even with a teacher's help
    4. The stage of development at which a child begins to reason about abstract ideas
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. The zone of proximal development is the range between what a learner can do alone and what is too hard even with help. Teaching is most effective inside the zone, using scaffolding, temporary support such as a hint or model that is removed as the learner becomes capable. The second and third options describe the areas below and above the zone. The last option describes Piaget's formal operational stage, a different theory.

    Concept Tested: Zone of Proximal Development

    See: How Students Learn: Four Theories

    2. How does Papert's constructionism differ from Piaget's constructivism?¶

    1. Constructionism says knowledge is received ready-made from a teacher
    2. Constructionism says learning happens only through conversation with peers
    3. Constructionism says learning is strongest when building a shareable artifact
    4. Constructionism says children pass through four fixed stages of thinking
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. Constructivism holds that learners build understanding by connecting new experiences to what they already know. Papert, a student of Piaget, added that people learn especially well when they make something tangible and shareable, such as a program, robot, or story, because the artifact makes thinking visible and can be examined and fixed. Learning through conversation is Vygotsky's social constructivism, and the four stages belong to Piaget. Neither theory says knowledge is received ready-made.

    Concept Tested: Constructionism

    See: How Students Learn: Four Theories

    3. According to the chapter, what simple test distinguishes a collaborative task from a cooperative one?¶

    1. Whether the teacher assigns the groups or students choose them
    2. Whether the final product is presented to an outside audience
    3. Whether every student receives the same grade for the project
    4. Whether the task requires students to talk with each other to succeed
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. In cooperation, people may divide a job into pieces and stick the pieces together, so each could finish alone. In collaboration, the thinking itself is shared, and the team decides together what the product must say. The chapter's test is whether the task requires students to talk with each other to succeed; if each could finish alone, it is cooperation at best. Group assignment, audience, and grading do not decide which kind of task it is.

    Concept Tested: Collaboration

    See: Worked Example: Cooperation Versus Collaboration

    4. During a project, a teammate says, "This is a waste of time. Our school will never change." Which reply is an example of active listening?¶

    1. "That's not true. The principal already said she likes our plan."
    2. "So you're worried our work won't lead to any real change?"
    3. "You should try to be more positive about the project."
    4. "Let's just vote and move on to the next task."
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. Active listening means attending fully, paraphrasing what you heard, asking clarifying questions, reflecting feelings, and holding back judgment and advice. The second reply paraphrases the concern, which shows the speaker was heard and invites more. The first reply argues and the third gives advice; the chapter notes these common, well-meant replies tend to end the conversation. Moving to a vote skips the concern entirely.

    Concept Tested: Active Listening

    See: Worked Example: Responses to a Teammate

    5. Robert Slavin's reviews of cooperative learning identify which two elements as mattering most?¶

    1. A shared group goal and individual accountability
    2. Competitive rankings and frequent teacher praise
    3. Mixed-age groups and a fixed team leader
    4. Group grades and a shared final presentation
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. Slavin's reviews found that a shared group goal and individual accountability matter most. Without a shared goal, a group becomes a collection of individuals; without individual accountability, one person can do all the work. The Johnsons list five elements, including these two plus face-to-face interaction, social skills, and group processing. Competitive rankings work against cooperative learning, and the chapter recommends rotating roles rather than a fixed leader.

    Concept Tested: Teamwork

    See: Collaboration, Teamwork, and Team Problem Solving

    6. Priya insists the team present with a video; Dev insists on a live demonstration. Following the Fisher and Ury approach, what should the team do first?¶

    1. Let the student who spoke first choose, so the team saves time
    2. Ask the teacher to pick, since the students cannot agree
    3. Combine both formats, so neither student feels they lost
    4. Ask each why they want that format, then seek a plan meeting both
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. Fisher and Ury's approach separates positions (what people say they want) from interests (why they want it). In the chapter's poster-versus-survey example, once the interests were on the table, a plan that met both was easy to find. Asking "why do you want that?" before arguing about "what," with active listening, gets an honest answer. Combining both formats is a compromise that may serve neither interest. Deferring to the first speaker or the teacher skips resolution.

    Concept Tested: Conflict Resolution

    See: Worked Example: Positions and Interests

    7. In two minutes, a student lists 10 uses for a paperclip. The ideas fall into 4 categories, and 3 of them were given by fewer than 5 percent of the class. What are the student's fluency, flexibility, and originality scores?¶

    1. Fluency 4, flexibility 10, originality 3
    2. Fluency 10, flexibility 3, originality 4
    3. Fluency 10, flexibility 4, originality 3
    4. Fluency 3, flexibility 4, originality 10
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. Fluency is the number of ideas (10), flexibility is the number of different categories (4), and originality is the number of ideas few others gave (3). The other options mix up the three measures. The chapter stresses that the scores are a conversation starter for coaching, not a verdict on a child's creativity; a student with high fluency and low flexibility, for example, can be asked for a completely different kind of use.

    Concept Tested: Divergent Thinking

    See: Worked Example: Scoring Divergent Thinking

    8. A team has argued for half an hour. One student has taken the keyboard and is doing everything, two have stopped contributing, and no one is running the discussion. What does the chapter's structural diagnosis point to?¶

    1. A lack of content knowledge, which more adaptive practice will fix
    2. Missing team roles and individual accountability
    3. Students who are not motivated and should be graded more strictly
    4. A team that is too small for the size of the project
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. In the food-waste team example, the problem was never knowledge; it was structure. One person doing everything with no facilitator signals missing team roles, and the silent members signal no individual accountability. The fix was to assign rotating roles such as facilitator, recorder, timekeeper, and reporter, and to give each member a part to own. The chapter advises asking the structural questions first instead of telling a stalled team to work harder.

    Concept Tested: Team Roles

    See: Worked Example: Rescuing the Food-Waste Team

    9. A teammate goes quiet after his project idea is not chosen. Which kind of empathy does the chapter say produces pro-social behavior, and what does it need?¶

    1. Cognitive empathy, which needs only a lesson on naming feelings
    2. Affective empathy, which needs a reward for each kind act
    3. Compassionate empathy, which needs the other two kinds plus a chance to act
    4. Perspective taking, which needs the student to agree with the teammate
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. The chapter distinguishes cognitive empathy (understanding what someone feels), affective empathy (feeling some of what they feel), and compassionate empathy (being moved to help). Compassionate empathy is the one that produces pro-social behavior, as when a teammate offers to use part of the quiet student's idea. It needs the other two plus a chance to act, which a team project provides. Rewards can turn kindness into a transaction, and perspective taking does not require agreement.

    Concept Tested: Empathy

    See: Worked Example: Three Kinds of Empathy

    10. A principal proposes an afternoon of free hands-on activity with no teacher guidance, citing a chart that says students remember 90 percent of what they do. What is the best response based on the chapter?¶

    1. The percentages are a myth; hands-on learning works when it is guided
    2. The chart is accurate, so the plan should go ahead as proposed
    3. Hands-on learning has no research support at all and should be dropped
    4. The plan works only for students in Piaget's formal operational stage
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. The chapter says Dale's Cone of Experience and its 10-percent and 90-percent figures are a myth; the percentages were added later and have no research behind them. Hands-on learning, rooted in Dewey's work, is effective when guided and tied to the concepts being learned, and much weaker when students simply do things without direction. The fraction-strip example worked because the teacher chose the materials and question. Dropping hands-on learning overcorrects.
