    Quiz: Sustaining Change: Systems Thinking and National Adoption¶

    Test your understanding of the later stages of change, culture assessment, systems thinking, and national AI adoption with these review questions.

    1. Bridges named four things leaders must supply for a new beginning. What are they?¶

    1. Pressure, praise, policy, and pay
    2. Pilot, proof, publicity, and permanence
    3. Purpose, picture, plan, and part
    4. People, process, products, and priorities
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. Bridges' four P's are the purpose behind the change, a picture of what the outcome looks and feels like, a plan for getting there, and a part for each person to play. The chapter calls the last the most neglected: a teacher who knows the plan but not her own role cannot begin. In the worked example, Mr. Hale's new beginning includes a named part, "reading lead for the small groups." The other options are not Bridges' list.

    2. In CBAM, which stages of concern are the impact concerns?¶

    1. Consequence, collaboration, and refocusing (stages 4 to 6)
    2. Awareness, informational, and personal (stages 0 to 2)
    3. Management only, the task concern (stage 3)
    4. Informational and management (stages 1 and 3)
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. Impact concerns are stages 4 to 6: consequence (are students benefiting?), collaboration (how can we work with colleagues to increase the benefit?), and refocusing (how could we improve or replace the innovation?). Stages 0 to 2 are self concerns, and stage 3, management, is the task concern about time, materials, and logistics. The chapter says a teacher with impact concerns is a leader's best asset, and answering task concerns well lets teachers move on to them.

    See: CBAM, Completed: Task and Impact Concerns, and Levels of Use

    3. A causal loop diagram links three variables in a circle. Two of the three arrows carry a minus sign, and one carries a plus sign. What kind of loop is it?¶

    1. Balancing, because it contains at least one minus sign
    2. Reinforcing, because it has an even number of minus signs
    3. Balancing, because it has more minus signs than plus signs
    4. Neither, because loops with mixed signs cannot be classified
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. The chapter's rule is to count the minus signs around a loop: an even number, including zero, makes it reinforcing, and an odd number makes it balancing. Two minus signs is even, so the loop is reinforcing. Two opposite-direction links cancel out, so a change still feeds back on itself in the same direction. The first and third options use rules the chapter does not give; any loop can be classified by this count.

    4. A district reports 100 percent of classrooms logging in to its new adaptive software. Why does the chapter warn that this may still be reform without real change?¶

    1. Because login data are usually incorrect and should not be collected
    2. Because full adoption always brings a dip that hides the real gains
    3. Because the software only counts teachers, not students, as its active users
    4. Because real change means practice differs, not just that the tool is running
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. Reform without real change is when an innovation is adopted in name but the actual work of teaching stays the same, as in the Eastside classroom where the software ran unattended while the teacher lectured. Larry Cuban documented this across a century as "oversold and underused." The chapter says a school can have 100 percent adoption on the dashboard and no change in practice, so leaders should watch what teachers and students do and compare it with the innovation configuration.

    5. A teacher says, "I use the new textbook every day, but I'm always scrambling to keep up and the logins keep failing." What stage of concern and level of use does this suggest, and what should a coach offer?¶

    1. Impact concern and refinement; a discussion of student outcomes data
    2. Self concern and nonuse; an introduction to what the textbook is
    3. Task concern and mechanical use; practical help with the logistics
    4. Collaboration concern and integration; time to plan with colleagues
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. The teacher uses the innovation daily but in a scrambling, step-by-step way, which matches mechanical use (level III), and the worry about fitting it in and failing logins is a task concern (stage 3, management). The chapter warns that a coach who answers this teacher with a lecture on student outcomes has answered a question she has not yet asked. She needs help with logistics. She is already using the textbook, so nonuse does not fit.

    6. What does Bridges' neutral zone describe, and how can leaders help during it?¶

    1. A pause before the change is announced; leaders should keep plans secret
    2. The confusing middle after the old way ends; leaders give temporary structure
    3. The final phase when the change is complete; leaders should celebrate and move on
    4. A period when no one is affected by the change; leaders need do nothing at all
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. The neutral zone is the middle phase of a transition: the old way has ended and the new one is not yet comfortable. People are confused, anxious, and less productive, and morale often dips. Bridges called it a time of danger and opportunity, since people may withdraw but may also be unusually creative. Leaders cannot skip it; they help with temporary structure, frequent communication, short-term goals, and treating the confusion as normal. New beginnings, not the neutral zone, are the final phase.

    7. A superintendent leads a district where three reforms in five years were dropped, and teachers say, "This too shall pass." Using the chapter's guide to choosing a change model, which approach should she lean on?¶

    1. Bridges' transition model, attention to change fatigue, and capacity building
    2. Kotter's eight steps alone, moving quickly to show real strength this time
    3. A rapid mandate with external accountability for every teacher
    4. Rogers' adopter categories, to identify the laggards and replace them
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. The chapter's guide says a school with a history of failed reforms should lean on Bridges, change fatigue, and capacity building, because people need their losses and the past honored. Change fatigue, which Abrahamson called "repetitive change syndrome," is an argument for fewer changes kept longer. Moving fast or imposing a mandate risks triggering the balancing loop of resistance. The book treats laggards' concerns with respect rather than as people to replace.

    8. How does Fullan and Quinn's coherence framework address change fatigue?¶

    1. By adding new initiatives each year so that staff stay energized
    2. By replacing internal responsibility with external accountability
    3. By letting each school choose its own unrelated priorities
    4. By focusing a system on a small number of clear, connected priorities
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. The coherence framework has four elements: focusing direction on a small number of clear priorities, cultivating collaborative cultures, deepening learning, and securing accountability with internal responsibility first and external second. A system asked to do twelve unconnected things will do none of them well, so coherence is the discipline of fewer, connected priorities. Adding initiatives is the cause of change fatigue, and the framework puts internal responsibility ahead of external accountability.

    9. A district's rollout is slower than planned, so a leader orders schools to speed it up. The rollout contains a loop in which rollout speed raises teacher overload (+), overload raises resistance (+), and resistance slows the rollout (−). What does systems thinking predict?¶

    1. The rollout will speed up steadily, since the loop is reinforcing
    2. The loop will stop working once the leader issues the order
    3. Pushing harder raises resistance; reducing overload works better
    4. Resistance will fall, since faster rollouts end the uncomfortable middle sooner
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. The loop has one minus sign, an odd number, so it is balancing: pushing the rollout faster raises overload and resistance, which push the rollout back. A leader who sees only the reinforcing loop of success will accelerate and be surprised. The chapter concludes that the system pushes back in proportion to the push, and that going slower on the push and faster on capacity, such as removing obstacles and reducing overload, is often the faster route.

    10. The chapter contrasts Estonia's AI Leap with South Korea's 2025 AI digital textbooks, as reported. What lesson does it draw?¶

    1. A rollout built on training and trust beats a rapid mandate that outruns capacity
    2. National programs always fail, so AI adoption should be left to individual teachers
    3. Larger countries adopt AI faster because they have bigger national technology budgets
    4. Digital textbooks succeed only when they replace printed textbooks entirely
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. Estonia paired AI tools with teacher training and built on earlier success with school technology, a coherent, capacity-building approach whose results are still unfolding. In South Korea, as reported, objections from teachers and parents about pace, screen time, privacy, and workload led the legislature within months to reclassify AI textbooks as supplementary materials. The chapter calls this contrast the lesson of the whole chapter. It does not say national programs always fail, and it notes both cases should be checked against current reporting.
