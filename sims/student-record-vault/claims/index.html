    Student Record Vault: Claim Audit¶

    Verification trail for the facts in data.json, following Phases 1-4 of the verified-infographic guide. Checked 2026-11-05. Notes paraphrase the source; they are not quotations.

    Claims¶

    # Claim (as used on the poster) Status Source Supporting note
    1 FERPA (1974) applies to schools and agencies that receive U.S. Department of Education funds verified S1, S2 34 CFR 99.1 limits Part 99 to agencies and institutions receiving Department funds; ED's FERPA FAQ describes it as a federal student privacy law
    2 Parents, and students who turn 18, may inspect records and ask for corrections verified S2 ED FAQ: rights to access and to request amendment; rights pass to the "eligible student" at 18 or on entering postsecondary study
    3 Consent is generally required before disclosing identifying information, with a school-official exception for vendors meeting three conditions verified S3 34 CFR 99.31(a)(1)(i)(B): outside party performs an institutional service, is under the school's direct control for use and maintenance of records, and follows the redisclosure limits of 99.33(a)
    4 A school must answer an inspection request within 45 days verified S4 34 CFR 99.10(b): within a reasonable time, not more than 45 days
    5 FERPA gives no individual right to sue (Supreme Court, 2002) verified (chapter only, not on poster) S5 Gonzaga Univ. v. Doe, 536 U.S. 273 (June 20, 2002): FERPA's nondisclosure provisions create no personal rights enforceable under 42 U.S.C. 1983
    6 COPPA (1998) protects children under 13 online; operators need notice and verifiable parental consent; enforced by the FTC verified S6 FTC COPPA FAQ states the Rule protects children under 13; original FTC rule effective April 2000 under the 1998 Act
    7 A school may consent on parents' behalf only for the school's educational purpose, not for commercial uses such as advertising or unrelated profiles verified S6 FTC COPPA FAQ section N: school authority limited to the educational context; operator using data for its own commercial purposes must get parental consent directly
    8 The COPPA Rule was amended in 2025 and now requires a written data retention policy verified S6, S7 FTC FAQ banner notes the Rule was amended April 22, 2025; law-firm alerts report the amendments took effect June 23, 2025 and require a written retention policy
    9 GDPR has applied since 25 May 2018 verified S8 European Commission: applies since 25 May 2018 (entered into force 24 May 2016)
    10 GDPR covers EU-established organizations and some non-EU organizations that offer services to, or monitor, people in the EU verified S9 Article 3(1) and 3(2)
    11 Top GDPR fines reach 20 million euros or 4% of worldwide annual turnover, whichever is higher verified S10 Article 83(5). Note: breach-notification failures (Art. 33) sit in the lower tier, 10 million euros or 2% (Art. 83(4))
    12 inBloom was publicly launched in 2013 with about $100 million from the Gates Foundation and the Carnegie Corporation verified, with nuance S11, S16 EdWeek gives $100 million from Gates and the Carnegie Corporation; Nonprofit Quarterly also reports a $100 million Gates-backed venture (Chalkbeat, S12, names only Gates). EdWeek dates the founding to 2011 (as the Shared Learning Collaborative); the public launch was February 2013. The poster says "publicly launched in 2013"
    13 Louisiana and a Colorado district withdrew in 2013; New York passed a law barring such data sharing in April 2014 verified S11, S12 EdWeek: Louisiana backed away May 2013, Jefferson County (CO) dropped out November 2013, New York legislature acted April 2014. Chalkbeat confirms the New York budget language ending the partnership less than a month before the shutdown; the Louisiana and Colorado details rest on EdWeek alone
    14 inBloom announced its shutdown in April 2014 verified S11, S12 EdWeek and Chalkbeat New York both report the April 21, 2014 wind-down announcement
    15 In late December 2024 an attacker used a single compromised credential to reach PowerSchool's customer-support portal (PowerSource) and took student and teacher data verified S13, S14 TechCrunch: a single compromised credential used on PowerSource in December 2024; EdWeek: the company's notice cites a compromised credential and a December 28 incident
    16 The portal did not have multi-factor authentication verified S13 PowerSchool told TechCrunch the portal did not support MFA at the time; the Texas Attorney General's lawsuit makes the same allegation (reported in search results)
    17 PowerSchool told customers in early January 2025 verified S13, S14 TechCrunch: first disclosed early January 2025; EdWeek (January 9, 2025): customers notified that week
    18 Number of students and teachers affected downgraded to "tens of millions", company did not confirm S13, S15 The 62.4 million students / 9.5 million teachers figures trace to the attacker's own claim as reported by BleepingComputer; TechCrunch repeats them and notes PowerSchool would not confirm. Not two independent confirmations, so no number appears on the poster
    19 CrowdStrike found earlier access in August-September 2024 removed S13 CrowdStrike could not confirm it was the same actor; not needed for the poster
    20 Chapter's illustrative content (Lincoln retention schedule, four-fifths audit, Maya, Mr. Chen, governance must-haves) cites the chapter Ch. 10, Ch. 8 Book's own worked examples; no external check needed

    Notes for the chapter¶

    • Chapter 10 says "The PowerSchool data breach came in December 2024. PowerSchool ... disclosed that attackers had gained access". The intrusion was in late December 2024, but the disclosure came in early January 2025 (S13, S14). Consider "came to light in January 2025".
    • The chapter hedges that the missing multi-factor authentication "should be checked against the latest reports". PowerSchool itself told TechCrunch the portal did not support MFA at the time (S13), so the hedge can be dropped.
    • The chapter says inBloom's funding came "mainly from the Gates Foundation"; both sources name Gates and the Carnegie Corporation of New York together.
