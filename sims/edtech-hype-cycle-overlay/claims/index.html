    The EdTech Hype Cycle: Claim Audit¶

    Audit trail for The EdTech Hype Cycle, from Phases 1 to 4 of the verified-infographic guide. Each callout tip in data.json cites the source_ids below. Wave placements on the curve follow Chapter 15's own text and worked-example table; they are a reading of the evidence, not a measurement. Notes are paraphrases, not quotations.

    Claims¶

    # Claim (as used in the poster) Status Source IDs Note
    1 The hype cycle was introduced by Gartner analyst Jackie Fenn in 1995 (chapter: "mid-1990s") verified S1, S2 Wikipedia and secondary methodology summaries agree on Fenn and 1995.
    2 Five stages: trigger, peak of inflated expectations, trough of disillusionment, slope of enlightenment, plateau of productivity verified S1, S2 Gartner's current pages use "Innovation Trigger"; the original name was "Technology Trigger". The poster uses the chapter's "innovation trigger" and notes the older name in a tip.
    3 The model is not a scientific law; many technologies do not follow it verified S1 Wikipedia summarizes analyses finding few technologies pass through an identifiable cycle.
    4 Edison predicted in a July 1913 interview that books would soon be obsolete in the public schools verified S3, S4 Quote Investigator traces it to the New York Dramatic Mirror, July 9, 1913; the issue is digitized at the Internet Archive.
    5 Film became a supplement, not a replacement for books downgraded S3 Kept as a qualitative statement; a 1913 trade editorial quoted by Quote Investigator already called film a supplement.
    6 Skinner proposed teaching machines in 1954 verified S5, S6 His 1954 paper "The Science of Learning and the Art of Teaching" launched the movement. Chapter 3 gives the same date.
    7 Interest in teaching machines faded by the late 1960s (costs, boredom, thin evidence) verified S5, S6 Both describe the late-1960s backlash and decline. This trough placement is an inference from the chapter's statement that each wave had a peak and a trough; the chapter does not name a stage for teaching machines.
    8 Ideas from teaching machines returned in computer-based learning verified S5, S25 Qualitative only.
    9 Apple II reached schools in 1977 verified (chapter) chapter 3, S7 Apple II released 1977; Minnesota's MECC chose it for state schools in 1978.
    10 MECC packaged software with guides for teachers new to computers verified S7, S8
    11 After the 1980s boom, a smaller group of teachers kept experimenting and developed effective practices downgraded chapter 15 The chapter's own event (practice sim, event 8). Kept qualitative; no numbers.
    12 "By the early 1980s many districts were buying microcomputers" removed — Not checked against two sources; replaced with the chapter's wording, "computers spread into classrooms in the 1980s".
    13 The New York Times called 2012 "the year of the MOOC" (November 2012) verified S9, S10 Laura Pappano's article, dated November 2 to 4, 2012 depending on edition.
    14 In 2013 Sebastian Thrun said Udacity had a "lousy product" verified S11, S12 Fast Company profile, November 2013, widely quoted.
    15 MOOC completion rates were often under ten percent verified S13, S14 Jordan (2014) found an average of 6.5%. Her 2015 follow-up found a median of 12.6% across 221 courses, so "often", not "always", is the right word.
    16 MOOCs settled into a niche in professional and continuing education downgraded chapter 15 Qualitative, from the chapter; Udacity's 2013 pivot to vocational training (S11) supports it.
    17 LAUSD began a plan in 2013 to give every student an iPad verified S15, S16 Cost figures differ across sources ($1 billion to $1.3 billion), so the poster gives no dollar amount.
    18 Within days, students at one high school bypassed the security settings verified S16 About 300 Roosevelt High students, within hours, September 2013. Poster says "within days" (conservative).
    19 After the FBI seized contract documents in December 2014, the district cancelled the contract verified S15, S16
    20 A federal inquiry later ended with no charges verified S17 Added for fairness, since the chapter calls it a contract scandal.
    21 ChatGPT released November 30, 2022 verified S18, S19
    22 NYC schools blocked ChatGPT in January 2023 and lifted the block in May 2023 verified S20 Several outlets (NBC, Bloomberg, Chalkbeat op-ed by Banks) agree. Supports the chapter's "schools ban, then rush to adopt".
    23 The company behind the Los Angeles district's chatbot collapsed in 2024 verified S23, S24 AllHere furloughed most staff in June 2024 and LAUSD took its "Ed" chatbot offline. Contract value differs across sources, so no dollar amount is given.
    24 A randomized study found open chatbot access lowered later exam scores, and a tutor with guardrails largely avoided that harm verified S21, S22 Bastani et al., PNAS 2025: about 1,000 Turkish high school math students. The chapter's "guided tutors help" is softened to "largely avoided that harm", since the guided group scored about the same as the control on the exam.
    25 Generative AI is "toward the trough"; the plateau is not yet reached downgraded chapter 15 The chapter's own reading, labelled as one on which reasonable people disagree.

    Summary: 19 verified, 5 downgraded to qualitative or chapter-sourced language, 1 removed. No numeric claim in the poster lacks a source.
