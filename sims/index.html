    List of MicroSims for AI-Native Schools¶

    Interactive MicroSims that let you try out the ideas in each chapter: classify real school decisions, run cost and growth calculators, and explore learning graphs. Each one is embedded in its chapter and also works on its own page.

    • Afternoon Program Quality Judge

      Judge six fictional afternoon programs as structured, partly structured, or unstructured by counting the SAFE practices each one includes.

    • AI Cost per Student Calculator

      Calculate the yearly AI inference cost per student from tokens per session, sessions per day, school days, and price per million tokens, then explore how price changes the result.

    • AI Terms Classifier

      Place eight example systems in the narrowest of six nested categories, from Not AI to AI agent.

    • AI Tutor Design Judge

      Judge six fictional AI tutor designs as likely to harm learning, mixed, or likely to help, by applying the five-guardrail rule.

    • Alpha or Unbound?

      Compare Alpha School and Unbound Academy by placing nine facts as true of Alpha only, Unbound only, or both.

    • Anatomy of a Learning Record

      Follow one click from a MicroSim to a teacher's dashboard: the xAPI statement, the Learning Record Store, the learning graph and the student's key.

    • Answer Machine vs. Thinking Tutor

      An interactive grid infographic of the same homework question handled two ways: an open chatbot that gives the answer and leaves the student with nothing to do, and a guided AI tutor that gives hints and leaves the student doing the thinking, with the guardrails that make the difference.

    • Artifact, Value, or Assumption?

      Sort eight statements about a school into Schein's three layers of culture: artifacts, espoused values, and basic assumptions.

    • Ask the Right Question

      Match six result claims to the question that best exposes the weakness in each.

    • Bloom Level Task Sorter

      Sort twelve classroom tasks into the six levels of the revised Bloom's Taxonomy, with a hint and a second try for each task.

    • Building Blocks Concept Map

      Explore how the 21 Chapter 1 concepts depend on one another, then select the direct prerequisites of six concepts on the map.

    • Challenge and Prior Knowledge Zone Explorer

      Predict whether a student is bored, engaged, in productive struggle, or overwhelmed from the gap between task challenge and prior knowledge, then explore the model.

    • Choose the Microstrategy

      Choose the microstrategy that best fits each of eight teacher situations, by diagnosing the concern first.

    • Choose the Move to Cross the Chasm

      Choose, for six situations, the move that best helps an innovation cross the chasm from early adopters to the early majority.

    • Coach Capacity Calculator

      Calculate the number of coaches a rollout needs, rounding up, and the number of teachers a given number of coaches can support.

    • Contract Clause Review

      Judge eight clauses from a fictional AI product contract as acceptable, needing change, or a red flag.

    • Cost per Student by Level

      Calculate the yearly cost per student and the district cost for Level 2, 3, and 4 textbooks from itemized costs, then explore AI usage, token price, and devices.

    • Crossing the Chasm

      Explore Moore's chasm as a landscape shaped like the adoption curve, with 13 callouts for the adopter groups, the whole product, the beachhead and the risk of crossing by hope.

    • Doubling Time Projection Calculator

      Calculate how many months a 50-minute task horizon takes to reach 40 hours for doubling times of 4, 5, and 6 months, then explore other doubling times and targets.

    • The EdTech Hype Cycle

      An interactive infographic of the hype cycle drawn as a landscape, with 11 callouts for the five stages and six waves of educational technology, from film in 1913 to generative AI.

    • Empathy or Not?

      Classify eight replies to a teacher who fears losing their job as dismissive, defensive, reassurance without substance, or empathic and substantive.

    • Equal vs. Equitable Tutoring

      Compare an equal split of 45 tutoring hours with an equitable one by finding the allocation that brings three students to a score of 80.

    • Evidence Tier Claim Checker

      Judge the evidence behind ten fictional vendor claims by placing each in one of five evidence levels, from Strong to No evidence of effect.

    • The Factory Day vs. the AI-Native Day

      An interactive poster of two school buildings in cross-section: the industrial-era factory day on the left and the AI-native day on the right, with six zones for the teacher's role, the morning and the afternoon in each school.

    • False Accusations Calculator

      Calculate how many human-written essays an AI detector wrongly flags, the share of flags that are false, and the share of false flags that fall on English-language learners.

    • Find the Missing Safeguard

      Read six pilot plans against the seven-risk pilot checklist and find the one risk each plan fails to address.

    • Five Levels: Textbooks and Self-Driving Cars

      An interactive grid poster that sets the six SAE J3016 levels of driving automation above the five levels of intelligent textbooks, with the shared Level 3 threshold and the rule for testing a vendor's level claim.

    • Force Field Balance

      Calculate the driving, restraining, and net forces for a school change, before and after reducing two restraining forces, then explore with sliders.

    • Four Thinking Skills Sorter

      Classify eight school activities as decomposition, pattern recognition, abstraction, or algorithmic thinking.

    • Four-Fifths Bias Audit Calculator

      Calculate selection rates and four-fifths ratios for three student groups, decide which groups an audit flags, then change the counts and watch the flags update.

    • Generate Once Versus Generate for Each Student

      Compare the yearly cost of generating a textbook once and serving it to everyone with generating content live for each student, and find the break-even number of students.

    • Governance Readiness Judge

      Judge six fictional districts as not ready, ready for Level 3, or well placed for Level 4 by applying the must-have and should-have rule.

    • Is the Gap Closing?

      Compute the gap between higher-income and lower-income students before and after for five schools, and decide whether each gap is closing, widening, or unchanged.

    • Is This a Normal Dip?

      Judge whether each of three pilot performance series shows a normal implementation dip, a deep or late dip, or no dip, using a stated rule.

    • Is This Data Identifying?

      Classify eight data fields as a direct identifier, identifying in combination, or not identifying.

    • Judging the Evidence for a Shorter Morning

      Judge the strength of the evidence behind eight claims about AI tutoring and the compressed morning, using the five evidence levels from Chapter 2.

    • Kotter's Eight Steps as a Mountain Trail

      Climb Kotter's eight steps for leading change as camps on a switchback trail, with two risks: skipping steps and declaring victory too soon.

    • Learning Graph Viewer

      Interactive viewer for the 532-concept AI-Native Schools learning graph, with search, category filtering, and statistics.

    • Level-Specific Safeguard Matcher

      Choose the lowest of five textbook levels at which each of eight safeguards should first be required.

    • Listening Responses

      Classify eight replies to a nervous teammate as paraphrase, clarifying question, reflecting feeling, advice, or judgment.

    • Mastery Threshold Chooser

      Examine a table of pass probabilities for five mastery rules and select every rule that meets each of four requirements.

    • Match Activity to Skill

      Classify ten afternoon activities by the human skill each one builds most directly: collaboration, communication, empathy, creativity, or computational thinking.

    • METR Measurement Workflow

      Read the six steps that turn AI task results into a 50 percent time horizon, then answer five questions about the steps.

    • Missing Project Element

      Find the one essential project element that each of six fictional project descriptions lacks.

    • Morning Time Budget

      Calculate the minutes, hours, and school days a compressed academic morning frees, and the speedup it requires, then explore the trade-off with sliders.

    • Name That Change Model

      Match each of eight school change situations to the change model that best addresses it.

    • One Lesson, Many Readiness Levels

      Predict where one whole-class lesson should be pitched for a class spread across eight readiness levels, then move the pitch to see who is in reach.

    • Prerequisite Chain Diagnosis

      Trace a student's failure on a fractions-to-proportions prerequisite map back to the root gap in five illustrative cases.

    • Product Level Classifier

      Judge the real level of eight fictional learning products and label each vendor claim an overclaim, accurate, or an underclaim.

    • Promise and Peril: Five AI-First Cases

      An interactive grid poster that reads five AI-first cases (Alpha School, Unbound Academy, the Turkish high school study, the Harvard physics AI tutor and Summit Learning) as a benefit, a risk and a response, with an empty band where no response was found.

    • Ready Concepts Finder

      Select exactly the ready concepts in a 16-concept learning graph for six different sets of mastered concepts.

    • Reinforcing or Balancing?

      Classify six causal loops from school change as reinforcing or balancing by counting the minus signs around each loop.

    • Rider, Elephant, or Path?

      Classify nine change tactics as directing the rider, motivating the elephant, or shaping the path, the three parts of the Switch framework.

    • The Rider, the Elephant and the Path

      Find the nine moves of the Switch framework in one scene: direct the rider, motivate the elephant and shape the path.

    • Scale, Extend, or Stop?

      Apply a pilot decision rule to six pilot results and judge whether each pilot should scale, extend, or stop.

    • Schein's Culture Iceberg

      Explore Schein's three levels of a school's culture as an iceberg, and see why reforms aimed at the visible tip can leave practice unchanged.

    • School Decision Stakeholder Match

      Match ten school decisions to the stakeholder who typically holds them: teacher, principal, district administrator, school board, or family.

    • Schooling and EdTech Timeline

      Explore twenty events in the history of schooling and educational technology from 1848 to 2022, then place eight of them in their eras.

    • Seat Time vs. Mastery

      Calculate how many of twenty students master a fixed-length course and how many hours the early finishers sit idle, then compare with a mastery-based schedule.

    • Seat Time vs. Mastery

      An interactive poster comparing two staircases of the same five concepts: seat time, where the calendar moves the class on and gaps crack the blocks, and mastery learning, where every block is solid but students spread apart in time.

    • Source Credibility Checker

      Rate eight fictional sources of evidence about an education product as Lead only, Use with caution, or Reasonable weight by applying a three-part rule.

    • Spacing and Retrieval Retention Explorer

      Predict which review schedule and method keep the most memory for a day-30 exam, then compare forgetting curves for massed, spaced, and expanding reviews.

    • The Student Record Vault

      An interactive infographic of who may come close to a student's record: a glowing folder inside four walls, for the family, the school, the district and vendors, with 14 callouts for FERPA, COPPA, GDPR, governance practices and the inBloom and PowerSchool cases.

    • Task Horizon Curve Explorer

      Calculate a model's chance of finishing tasks of different lengths from its 50 percent time horizon, then move the horizon and watch the success curve shift.

    • Tutor Reply Hint Levels

      Classify eight AI tutor replies to the same juice-price question by hint level, from 1 (asks a question) to 4 (gives the answer).

    • Vendor Scorecard

      Calculate the weighted scores of two vendors and apply the knockout rule to decide which vendor may proceed.

    • Verify the AI Summary

      Judge whether each of six statements in an AI-written summary is supported by a short source passage.

    • What a Textbook Costs per Student

      An interactive poster that answers a finance director's question with four stacks of cost blocks, print and Levels 2 to 4, drawn to scale, with the faded laptop a school may also have to buy standing behind them.

    • What Level of Use Is This?

      Classify eight teacher behaviors by CBAM level of use, from nonuse to renewal.

    • Where Is It on the Hype Cycle?

      Place eight events from the history of educational technology on the five stages of the hype cycle.

    • Where the Minutes Go in a Traditional School Day

      Estimate how many minutes of an illustrative 425-minute middle-school day are whole-class direct instruction, then reveal a chart of where the minutes go.

    • Which Attribute Is the Barrier?

      Name the innovation attribute that is the barrier behind each of eight teacher comments about a new tool.

    • Which Benefit or Risk?

      Distinguish eight statements from AI-first school stories as one of two benefits or one of six risks.

    • Which Concern Is This?

      Classify six teacher statements as awareness, informational, or personal concerns, and read the response that answers each one.

    • Which Kotter Step Is This?

      Classify eight leadership actions from one school's change effort by the Kotter step each carries out.

    • Which Learning Theory?

      Classify eight classroom scenarios as constructivism, social constructivism, constructionism, or social constructionism.

    • Which Lesson Does the Failure Teach?

      Match seven real failures of technology-first school programs to the lesson each one teaches.

    • Who May See This Record?

      Judge whether each of eight requests to see or use a student's learning record should be allowed, allowed only with an agreement and family notice, or refused.

    • Why Is This Team Stuck?

      Attribute the main cause of a student team's difficulty in six short vignettes to one of five causes.
